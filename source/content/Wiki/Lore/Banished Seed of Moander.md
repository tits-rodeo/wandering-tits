A seed of [[Moander]] was never destroyed during the god-wars of the Netheril era — only exiled, bound for the Astral Sea and deliberately delivered into the maw of an astral dreadnought. For centuries it was nothing more than a captive battery for [[Ashyra Veil]], an ur-priest who found it and cultivated it as her only source of power — until it finally infected her, and her own last act stopped it from becoming whole. What she left behind instead is spread through the entire donjon: neither a contained seed nor a true avatar, just an unfinished, out-of-control corruption with nowhere left to go.

## (DM Only) The Dark of It

### Origin
During the ancient rivalry between [[Moander]] and [[Jergal]] (dated to the Netheril era), a seed of Moander was seized intact by Jergal's agents — likely elements of the undead army Jergal raised specifically to scour the multiverse for the lost Code of Reversion. The seed could not be destroyed outright (canonically, only holy water and a blessing reliably kills one, and this one may already have grown past that threshold), so Jergal's agents chose containment over destruction.

Rather than banishing it at random, Jergal's forces had already located and tracked a specific astral dreadnought. The banishment was aimed deliberately at its vicinity, with full knowledge that the dreadnought would swallow the drifting seed and seal it inside its Demiplanar Donjon — an indestructible pocket demiplane, enterable only by *wish* or the dreadnought's own Donjon Visit ability, and escapable only by planar magic. From the perspective of whoever executed this containment, it would have looked like a permanent solution: better than risking a destruction attempt that might release the seed's essence, and effectively unreachable forever.

### Growth inside the Donjon
Unplanted and with no host to infect, the seed should have simply lain inert. Instead, sustained by the donjon's strange metaphysics — a demiplane that, per the dreadnought's own nature, is full of the decaying remains of centuries of "meals" — it slowly drew on the ambient organic matter around it and persisted, gaining a faint, passive vitality: not an infection spreading, just a dormant nodule of Moander's consciousness slowly thickening in place, gaining strength the same way the god's surviving consciousness was said to linger and feed at Darkwatch after his "death" at the hands of Finder Wyvernspur. For centuries, this is all it ever was — dormant, contained, harmless to anything that didn't go looking for it.

### The ur-priest
[[Ashyra Veil]], fleeing all divine attention by hiding in the one place in the multiverse no god could sense, eventually found her way into the donjon and discovered the dormant seed-nodule. Cut off from the wide, cautious, many-god theft her kind normally practices, she recognized it as a captive source of real divine power and began deliberately cultivating it — feeding it whatever organic remains she could find or drag to it from elsewhere in the donjon, keeping it alive and slowly growing so that it would keep functioning as a renewable divine battery rather than a source she could exhaust.

For most of that time, the arrangement held: she remained wholly herself, a caretaker and parasite standing apart from what she was draining, not a host merging with it. Centuries of a single, unbroken siphon (rather than the wide, careful sips her kind normally takes) warped her own mind long before it ever touched her body — an unquiet, prophecy-touched intelligence, still recognizably human in shape, but no longer entirely sane after so long alone with nothing but a rotting god-fragment for company.

Eventually, her own long isolation and negligence let it slip past her control. The seed finally did what it should have done centuries earlier and began infecting her directly, its rot spreading through her body the way it should have spread through any host from the start. Ashyra recognized what was happening before it could finish — and rather than let it complete and rise as a true avatar of Moander wearing her body, she killed herself. It was enough to stop the seed from ever becoming whole. It was not enough to undo what her death interrupted: with no host to finish forming in and no way to simply stop, the half-completed transformation spread outward instead, bleeding into the donjon itself. That uncontrolled, unfinished spread — neither a contained seed nor a real avatar — is what has since worked its way through the whole demiplane.

### The lead-lined portal room
The gate Tok would eventually use was never something Ashyra found — she built it herself, over ages of waiting for the dreadnought to swallow the specific components she needed to construct it. Once it existed, she sealed it inside a chamber of her own making — walls and a door lined in lead, meant to keep out unwanted visitors while she tended the seed nearby. Dense metals like lead and gold are established (if older/obscure) D&D lore as a barrier to ethereal passage — thick lead lining was a known ward against ethereal intrusion, scrying, and teleportation, historically used in palaces, mage towers, tombs, and vaults for exactly this purpose.

That safeguard outlived its purpose. Per the demiplane's local **Near Ethereal** (see below), Ashyra's ghost should have been able to phase through ordinary walls to reach her own portal room — except this room's lead lining locks it out just as effectively as it would any other ethereal intruder. Her own precaution became the one thing standing between her and the way out, for as long as the room stayed sealed. It only stopped mattering once someone opened the door from the material side: [[Kabuki Tok Tok|Tok]] found the room, and later stole the portal key that had kept it secured — the same act that let the ghost finally slip loose.

The gate itself isn't keyed to an object, or even a state of mind — it's keyed to a symbol: broken faith. Ashyra built her sanctum to recognize and welcome only what she herself had become — her own life is that concept given form, faith bent and broken rather than kept. She never needed a token to pass her own gate; she simply *was* the thing it was built to answer to. Her holy symbol of [[Hecate]] — a setting moon, warped and bent (whole, never shattered into pieces) the day her own faith turned on her — is that concept made into an object, which is why carrying it is enough for anyone else, Tok included, with no understanding of what it actually represents or why it works.

### The donjon's Near Ethereal
The Demiplanar Donjon isn't connected to the wider Ethereal Plane the way an ordinary location on the Material Plane is. Following the same precedent used for the Domains of Dread (Ravenloft/Barovia) in official lore — a sealed demiplane can have its own **local pocket of the Border Ethereal**, fully functional for anything happening inside it (a ghost here can manifest, phase through walls and debris, and possess normally), but with no connection outward to the Deep Ethereal that would otherwise let an ethereal traveler cross between worlds. That's why Ashyra's ghost could haunt the donjon freely for centuries but never simply phase her way out through the walls of the demiplane itself — the only real way out was always the gate.

### Research trips to Faerûn
Once her gate existed, Ashyra was no longer entirely sealed in. Powerful and practiced enough to slip in and out of Faerûn quickly and unseen — never long enough to draw the attention of the gods still hunting her — she used it for quiet research trips of her own. On one such trip she reached the ruins of a spell weaver colony allied with the gold dwarves of the Great Rift, and found something there naming a threat larger than anything she'd ever been hunted for: [[Atropus]]. Her knowledge of Atropus is learned, not prophetic — she read or was told something specific at those ruins, rather than foreseeing it herself.

### Current status
Ashyra died by her own hand rather than let the seed complete inside her, and rose afterward as a ghost — her fixation on stopping the seed, and on the escape she never finished, serving as whatever unfinished business anchors her. Whether her death was also what ruptured the seed loose and let it start spreading through the donjon, or whether that took further time afterward, isn't pinned down — either way, she escaped through her own gate (or was disturbed loose) around the time it came back into use. The donjon itself is now steeped in the seed's half-formed corruption throughout, a standing excuse for hazards, creeping decay, and disturbing horrors wherever the party goes, with no need to fix an exact single answer for what it's "become." She is capable of briefly possessing a living humanoid host and, while in control, has been observed to force out warnings drawn from what she learned at the spell weaver ruins — including a forewarning of the coming of [[Atropus]]. It is not Atropus and does not appear to be identical with [[Prophecy of the Stillborn Brother|the Stillborn Brother]] — she's relaying secondhand knowledge of Atropus's approach, among other things, rather than being that entity itself.

### Open threads
- Jergal's faction has no reason to know the containment ever failed — nobody has had cause to check on it in centuries, and nothing about Ashyra's death or escape would have reached them. As far as they're concerned, this matter has been closed for a very long time.
- Whether Moander's current-day resurgence could ever take an interest in this fragment if its existence became known is an open hook.

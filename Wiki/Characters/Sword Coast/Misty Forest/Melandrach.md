># Melandrach
>###### Stats
>Type|Stat|
>---|---|
>**Race**|Wild Elf|
>**Class**|Fighter/Druid|

Self-styled "King of the Woods," King of the [[Misty Forest]] and delegate for the elves of both the Misty Forest and the High Forest. Father of [[Alagarthas]] and [[Neronvain]]. Has led the Misty Forest's elves for over 150 years, since before most of their kind abandoned the realm in the Retreat, a decision he considered rash despite the years of discussion and meditation that preceded it.

## Personality
Quiet and arrogant. Where his sons Alagarthas and Neronvain always rushed headlong into their own pursuits, Melandrach is circumspect, hesitant to commit to any sudden course of action. His ideals: "We elves were once the greatest civilization to grace Faerûn, and my people are heir to that history. I will not squander our waning strength."

## History
When Delaan Winterhound of the Emerald Enclave raised concerns about renewed dragon attacks in the Misty Forest, Melandrach waved them away, insisting his elves' increased patrols and fortified positions had already ended the threat. He continued to argue for caution and more information at council, unmoved by any argument put to him, until it came to light that the green wyrmspeaker responsible for those very attacks was his own son Neronvain, long believed dead. Melandrach met the news with cold fury.

Afterward his pragmatism sharpened rather than softened. He remained unwilling to put his own elves on the front lines, but dropped his resistance to securing allies like Red Wizards, giants, or even devils, so long as they were arrayed in front of his elves in battle rather than beside them. He was also pressed into publicly apologizing for the Dracorage mythal, an old elven atrocity against dragonkind, an apology he made because honor demanded it while resenting the party for forcing his hand.

By the campaign's end, Melandrach is grateful for the adventurers' actions on his people's behalf. That gratitude sits uneasily alongside the shame and rage of learning what his exiled son had become.

The narrow street around the party is lined with clay and stone buildings, their facades weathered and damp. People move past without much interest. Some head to work, others finish night shifts. The air smells of dust, baked earth, and smoke. Water drips from a broken gutter. A few tents cluster in the shadows between buildings.

Close by, one building's door suddenly bursts outward. A figure crashes onto the cobblestones. A patron, dazed and bleeding. From inside the tavern comes the sound of breaking furniture, shouting, and the unmistakable crash of combat. Above the chaos, a single voice bellows out unhinged enthusiasm.*

"Crimson Slash!" Another crash of glass.

"Midnight Reversal!" The sound of wood splintering.

"Dragon's Ascent!" A roar of pain from someone.

Then, louder and more violent than the rest: "GLORY HOLD!" 

The tavern's heavy oak door is scarred and warped. A faded sign hangs from a wrought-iron bracket above it, showing a brick colored red by flame. Through the open doorway, shadows move and collide. More furniture crashes. The smell of cheap ale, cooking oil, tobacco smoke, and now blood pours out onto the street.

This... is the Burnt Brick.

More folks fly out the front door of the tavern. Most appear to have a slightly demonic aspect: small horns, or reddish skin, forked tails, and almost all have a slight smell of brimstone to them. The street is gradually filling with more and more injured tavern patrons.

Then a figure emerges from the tavern, grappling with another patron. A man with wild reddish-orange hair swept up in a high ponytail, an eyepatch covering one eye. His face is alive with an unhinged grin. He drags the struggling patron out onto the street with one hand. His peg leg thumps against the cobblestones as he pivots. A massive tetsubo suddenly appears in his free hand, materialized from nowhere. He swings it in a smooth arc. The blow connects with a sickening thud. The patron crumples.

The man stands over the fallen figure, laughing. He doesn't notice the party at first, but as his gaze sweeps across the street, he locks onto them. His grin widens.

"More blood for the Brick!" he shouts, and gestures broadly at the chaos. "Come on then, new faces. This is the best part of morning in Sigil. Show me what you've got!"

Before anyone can move, a wooden chair comes flying out of the tavern doorway. It catches him across the back and shoulders. He crumples to the cobblestones. The chair clatters beside him. Dazed, groaning.

From inside the tavern, a gruff voice bellows: "That's enough of your bullshit, One-Eye! Get out and take your mess with you!"

Silence. The man lies in the street, stunned. The brawl continues inside, but out here, the challenge has ended before it began.

---

The man sprawled on the cobblestones groans. When the party approaches and someone tells him it was a chair, he stares blankly for a moment. He then grabs said chair and hurls it over his shoulder, whereupon it simply vanished.

He keeps his weapons in a kind of extradimensional personal storage, it turns out, something he call "Hammer Space." He introduces himself as [[Corvus]], which is where his name begins and ends, apparently.

Before introductions go any further, however, [[Francis]] and [[Jig]] have already spotted something strange across the street: a figure standing utterly still among the foot traffic, watching the party. The face is unmistakably [[Jig]]'s — same build, same bearing, same everything. The moment their eyes meet, the figure steps backward into a doorway and vanishes.

Corvus is disinterested in mysteries but very interested in getting his dented tetsubo repaired. [[Mina]] offers to fix it, if they can find her grandfather's smithy in the Market Ward. That gets his attention. He knows someone who can help locate it, an information broker, which suits the party's reasons for coming to Sigil in the first place.

But first, the doorway.

The building the double vanished into takes some effort to enter, the door barred from the inside. After break the door down with brute force, what they eventually find inside is a single dusty room, completely vacant, without so much as a footprint in the grime. The double was never inside. A Detect Magic spell discovers a warding aura on the door: not a trap, but something more like a silent alarm. The party leaves with more questions than answers.

---

Corvus leads them through the Sandstone District of the Clerks' Ward to a shop whose sign reads **FORK OFF** in large letters, with *tuning forks & planar goods* in small print below. Inside, every available surface is covered in tuning forks — organized by plane, sorted by material, stuffed into jars and bins. One jar is labeled *Bargain Bin*. It also contains tuning forks.

Behind the desk sits a gnome of approximately three feet radiating a presence twice his height. Wild hair rises from his head in tentacle-like waves. A monocle, a jeweler's loupe on an armature, a staff that is itself a giant tuning fork — and several smaller forks orbiting his head. His name is [[Damroodleboss Delimimus]], though everyone calls him .

He already knows the smithy's name and marks it on a map. But the information he holds about a geas, or answers to other questions the party has been quietly carrying, will cost something first. His warehouse has gone missing again.

It tends to wander between the Clerks' and Market Wards, the thing apparently having some manner of sentience. Boss hands them a tuning fork made of mismatched metals that shimmers like oil on water. It *should* resonates in sympathy with the warehouse's hum. They'll know it when they hear it.

---

The walk to the Market Ward takes about an hour. Streets grow more colorful as they go; the gray shades and perfect right angles of the Clerks' Ward dissolves into open noise, crowds, and the sheer variety of every species Sigil has to offer: squid-headed scholars, strolling demons, haloed celestials. And nobody stares.

[[Bashers for Bashers]] is marked by a sign featuring two crossed clubs. Inside, they find [[Jedediah Stonewood]], a stout halfling in his late sixties with iron-gray hair in a ponytail, heavily scarred hands missing the pinky on his right. He works the forge. He spots Mina, and what follows is an argument (during which Mina's accent strangely changes) that ends in a hug. Grateful for her safe return, Jedediah presents the party with a sculpture he's kept on hand: *The Burnt Offering*, a fourteen-inch figure of spiraling flames in infernal green steel, crusted with rubies and garnets. He made it to express himself. He can make more, he says with a shrug, as if it was not a big deal and no great effor.

The tetsubo goes on the anvil. Corvus and Mina stay. The rest head out with a masterwork sculpture and a referral to the art dealer [[Marcus]] in the Great Bazaar.

---

Marcus, a kuo-toa in fine clothes examining art through a monocle, offers ten thousand gold after thoroughly studying the sculpture, pegging the price lower because the party mentioned it might be part of a series (though it wasn't meant to discourage him). The deal is struck.

With money to spend, the party works their way through a circuit of referred shops.

[[The Earthen Vault]], an armor shop that partially phases into the ground, run by Crack, a xorn whose gems are embedded in its stony hide. 

[[The Eyrie]], accessible only through a portal hidden on a wall, where a magnificent feathered serpent sells wondrous items from coiled repose.

[[The Honored Blade]], run by Caravox, a rakshasa with backwards hands and a palpable aura of evil, though he doesn't attack anyone.

And [[The Spore Garden]], tended by a myconid called The Gardener, smelling of earth and old medicine.

Somewhere outside the Earthen Vault, the Jig-lookalike appears again, face at the window. Jig charges, but the figure retreats. When pressed, all Jig can say is: *I'll explain. I just don't know how yet.*

---

A tiefling street urchin named Lyra has heard the warehouse's hum before. She leads them through the warehouse district, the multicolored tuning fork resonating louder with every block, until it nearly hums on its own.

And there, at the entrance of the warehouse, stands the double once again. He holds Jig's gaze for a long moment, then turns and vanishes around the corner.

Jig shouts, but the figure is gone.

The warehouse requires Corvus's key. [[Francis]] compresses their location into twenty-one words via Sending, and a few hours later as the ambient light of Sigil's sky begins to dim toward something like evening, Corvus arrives, key in hand.

Inside: floor-to-ceiling shelves of tuning forks organized by plane. Further, a spartan room with a weapon rack that clearly belongs to Corvus. 

One door leads to an area that grows as Conrad steps inside, doors emerging from the walls, among with other amenities and a kitchen.

One hallways stands out in particular, with five doors total (not counting the entrance). Each door opens onto somewhere else entirely: A howling lightless tunnel, a peaceful island in a calm river where the air smells of flowers and seems to forgive something, a steel chamber overlooking interlocking gears, a competitor's shop full of portal keys. 

And at the end of the hallway, behind a great bolted wheel-door, a spherical room with porthole windows looking out on *nothing* — not darkness, but a void the mind refuses to fully process.

Corvus notes that all the rooms in that hallway occasionally change the locations to which they lead, with the exception of the wheeled door at the end -- that particular room never changes. He doesn't know why it's there.

When they report the warehouse's condition to Boss via Sending. He pauses at the mention of the growing room Conrad entered, then replies that the warehouse appears to have created new living quarters for them, specifically.

"Sounds like an invitation," he says. "Though I haven't hired you... yet."

He instructs them to find a small amber bead beneath his office desk and place it inside a dismantled crate in the storage area. When they do, a portal opens _downward_ back to Boss's shop across the city, the two spaces blinking at each other.

Boss looks up from below.

"So then. What do you want to know?"

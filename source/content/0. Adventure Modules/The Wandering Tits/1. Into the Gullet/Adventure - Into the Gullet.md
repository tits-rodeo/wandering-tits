## Opening scene -- a night at The Wandering Tit
> *The Wandering Tit is doing brisk business tonight -- every table full, lantern-light catching in tankards, the low roar of a good crowd already half-drunk on the promise of a show. The bar itself is an old ship's timber, silvered and salt-worn, more character than the building around it deserves. Somewhere behind it, pots clatter. Somewhere in front of it, a lute is being tuned, badly, on purpose, to laughter.*

This scene exists to introduce the party in their element, establish the tavern's regulars (several of whom become tonight's stakes later), and let each PC have a small, character-defining beat before everything goes wrong. Take as much time here as the table wants -- there's no clock yet.

- **The room:** among tonight's crowd are the regulars who matter later -- **Old Maud** fussing over a mending basket in the corner, **Tobin** ferrying drinks and tripping over his own bootlaces, **Garrek** nursing a drink alone and working up the nerve to talk to Trixie, and **Renn Vask** holding court a little too loudly at a table of his own, already three drinks in. None of this needs to be flagged as significant -- just color, for now.
- **[[Azhura]] -- the rude encounter:** Azhura carries an order over to a rowdier table -- a group who've clearly decided the help exists to be mocked. Play it as petty and beneath her rather than dangerous: a snide comment about her draconic blood, maybe a "joke" at her expense that lands wrong, nothing worth drawing a blade over, but enough to needle a paladin's patience early in the night. Resolve however the player wants to play it -- ignore it, shut it down with a look, or let it simmer.
- **[[Mantis]] -- behind the bar:** Mantis takes the order at the bar (or fields a new one from someone more interesting than the usual crowd -- your call, or the player's, on who). A good spot for a small character beat: a customer who recognizes something about her weapon collection, asks about her homeworld, or just tries and fails to make small talk with a gladiator who isn't in the mood for it tonight.
- **[[Trixie]]'s first performance:** She takes the small stage first -- something upbeat, crowd-pleasing, exactly what a room like this wants. This is the "everything is normal" beat: let her actually land the performance, get the applause, feel good for a minute. It makes the interruption during her *second* set hit harder later.
- **[[Tok Tok]]'s kabuki performance:** Between sets, Tok Tok does his own strange, striking bit -- stylized, theatrical, a little too intense for a tavern crowd that doesn't quite know what to make of it, but charming for exactly that reason. A good beat for the party to see him as he wants to be seen: a performer, not yet the man who's about to be dragged back into the one place he's spent decades trying to forget.
- Let the scene breathe before moving to Trixie's second set and the interruption -- this is the last "normal" the party gets.

#### Tavern patrons (donjon fodder)
If the party tries to save any patrons during the Crimson Flame attack, some may end up dragged through the portal with them -- these are meant to die in the keep for horror impact, not to be long-term cast members. Mostly likeable on purpose, so the deaths actually land.

##### Old Maud
> *Human, stooped, silver hair in a kerchief, flour-dusted apron*
- **Trope / Character Reference:** Molly Weasley energy -- warm, mothering, will absolutely try to fuss over the party's wounds mid-crisis.
- **Voice:** Soft, fussy, calls everyone "dear" or "love" regardless of age or size.
- **Memorable Tidbit:** Insists on carrying a basket of mending/knitting with her even while fleeing for her life.
- **Home:** Lives above the bakery next door to the tavern; everyone in town knows her.
- **Purpose:** The gut-punch death -- the one the players actually liked, gone abruptly.

##### Tobin
> *Halfling, wide-eyed, patched vest, permanently untied bootlaces*
- **Trope / Character Reference:** Samwise Gamgee -- earnest, loyal, braver than he looks, wants desperately to help and keep everyone together.
- **Voice:** Eager, a little breathless, talks himself into courage out loud.
- **Memorable Tidbit:** Immediately attaches himself to whichever party member seems kindest, and won't leave their side.
- **Home:** Stable hand / errand runner for the tavern, practically grew up there.
- **Purpose:** Endearing enough that his death (or a moment where the party has to choose to save him or not) actually costs the players something.

##### Garrek
> *Half-orc, enormous, gentle eyes, carries a battered lute case he can't actually play*
- **Trope / Character Reference:** Hagrid -- huge, soft-spoken, kind to a fault, everyone assumes he's dangerous until he proves otherwise.
- **Voice:** Low, slow, gentle. Apologizes for taking up space.
- **Memorable Tidbit:** Was trying to work up the nerve to ask Trixie for lute lessons tonight.
- **Home:** Local dockhand, tavern regular, well-liked despite his size scaring off strangers.
- **Purpose:** A big, sympathetic presence that makes his fate feel disproportionately cruel -- great for a death that turns the mood of the whole session.

##### Renn Vask
> *Human, broad-shouldered, cocky grin, too many rings*
- **Trope / Character Reference:** Biff Tannen from *Back to the Future* -- a bully who's used to being the biggest threat in the room and hasn't learned otherwise yet.
- **Voice:** Sneering, confrontational, postures even when it's clearly a bad idea.
- **Memorable Tidbit:** Picks a fight with something in the donjon he has no business provoking. Always carries a **Charlatan's Die** he uses to cheat at dice -- recoverable from his body if he dies.
- **Home:** Local tough, small-time enforcer for nobody in particular, just likes being feared.
- **Purpose:** The one unlikeable one -- a death (or a moment of cowardice) the party might feel *less* bad about, for contrast against the others.

## The Crimson Flame attack
> A window shatters inward, and flame pours through it -- not orange, but a deep, wrong crimson, crawling up the walls like it's hunting rather than spreading.

- **Combatants:** 2x Cultist Fanatic ([[Crimson Flame]]) engage the party directly.
	- One opens with *Flaming Sphere* and tries to keep its distance, rolling the sphere in to harass from range.
	- The other opens with *Flame Blade* and goes straight into melee.
- **[[Tok Tok]]** shouts for the party to hold the fanatics off while he gets the tavern patrons into his "safe room" (the portal) -- this is what buys time for any patrons the party chooses to drag along.
- **Alchemical crimson fire:** [[Azhura]] recognizes it on sight -- this is the signature weapon of [[Crimson Flame]] raids, an alchemical concoction rather than ordinary flame.
	- Anything that starts its turn in the fire, or enters/ends its turn there, takes **2d6 fire damage + 2d6 necrotic damage**.
	- Spreads along flammable material rather than staying contained to where the window broke -- good pressure to keep the party moving toward the safe room rather than standing to fight it out.

## The Sealed Chamber (arrival in the donjon)
> The portal winks out behind you into a room lined wall-to-ceiling in dull grey lead. Whatever built this space meant it to keep something out -- or in. The only door hangs ajar, its old fortifications torn down from the inside; someone -- or something -- forced its way in. Crates and oddments are stacked against the walls -- someone's stash, forgotten mid-use. In the center of the room, cracked clean through, sits a strange crystal throne.

- This is [[Ashyra Veil|Ashyra]]'s old sanctum from centuries ago -- the lead-lined portal room. The barricade across the door was torn down from the inside, not broken in from outside: this is how her ghost got out.
- **Tok's stash:** whatever mundane/minor-magic-item dressing you want here -- gear he's squirreled away over the years, nothing urgent, just color and maybe a couple of usable minor items. Includes a six-pack of **Potions of Healing (Greater)** (heals 4d4 + 4), tucked away for exactly the kind of emergency this is.
- **The shattered crystal throne:** not Ashyra's -- this is the helm from [[Mantis]]'s crashed spelljammer craft, which Tok has been secretly holding onto for her. Pure atmosphere/mystery for tonight; no explanation given yet, just something clearly out of place that the party has no reason to understand.
- **The portal key:** not a literal key, but a holy symbol of [[Hecate]] -- a setting moon, badly bent and warped but still in one piece -- ornate and clearly valuable, belonging to a faith no one present recognizes. Tok found it deep in the keep on his first traversal of the donjon decades ago, and grabbed it as much for its obvious worth as anything else. In the chaos of arrival, the [[Husk Rat|husk rat]] snatches it from him right here in the chamber and darts out through the open door with it -- the party's reason to move, and their first look at what's living in this place.
	- **(DM only) Why it works as a key at all:** the gate isn't keyed to an object or a state of mind -- it's keyed to a symbol: broken faith. [[Ashyra Veil|Ashyra]] built her sanctum to recognize and welcome only what she herself had become, so she never needed a token to pass her own gate -- she simply *was* the thing it answers to. Her bent, broken holy symbol is that same concept made into an object, which is why holding it is enough for someone like Tok, who has no idea what it actually represents or why it works.
- While the party is still taking in the room (searching the stash, reacting to the rat, etc.), a loud crash of debris sounds from beyond the door -- startling, disorienting, no mechanical effect, just noise that makes them wonder exactly where they've ended up before they've even stepped outside.
- **The single door**, standing open, leads out -- the cavern reveal happens on stepping through it, not on arrival.

## The Courtyard (beyond the sealed chamber's door)
> Outside, the lead-lined chamber turns out to be the base of an obelisk -- tall, black, carved edge to edge in runes that seem to shift if you look at them straight on, its surface reflecting shapes that aren't standing anywhere nearby. Ringing it at a distance, a loose circle of squat, rune-carved stone stubs juts from the ground, and within that ring the ground is bare and quiet. Past it, strung between makeshift poles and leaning spars, a haphazard web of chains, ship's rigging, and rotten sailcloth stretches overhead, sagging under the weight of whatever's landed in it. Beyond all of that -- barely visible in the gloom -- something vast is moving.

- The **obelisk** is the outside of the Sealed Chamber -- the one piece of real craftsmanship in all of this, built with *fabricate* by [[Ashyra Veil|Ashyra]] herself. Nothing about it should look scavenged or improvised, in deliberate contrast to everything else the party is about to see.
- The **ring of obelisk-stubs** is a ward, an extension of the same working that lines the chamber in lead -- it keeps the worst of the donjon's corruption from encroaching on the courtyard. The blight visibly thins out or recoils at its edge rather than stopping at a hard line; there's no visible wall, just a boundary nothing crosses.
- The **chain-and-rigging net**, salvaged from wrecked ships and wagons like the ones visible in the wider cavern, is rigged to catch whatever drops from above when the dreadnought swallows something elsewhere -- this is the apparatus [[Ashyra Veil|Ashyra]] used for centuries to gather the specific components she needed to build the gate in the first place. Old, sagging, and full of debris nobody's collected in a long time.
- Past the ward and the netting, the wider cavern opens up: too large to be a cave, ceiling a thousand feet up and out of sight, walls ridged and wet like the inside of a throat. 2-3 pieces of half-buried wreckage are visible from here (a broken wagon, a suit of ancient plate, a smashed ship's hull) -- mostly pure atmosphere, but the smashed ship's hull still has an **Everburning Candle of the Deep** wedged in its old lamp-fitting, still lit, untouched by however long it's been down here.
- Good spot for a passive Perception/Wisdom (Survival) call: distant movement in the dark beyond the ward's edge. Nothing attacks yet -- just enough to make them want to get inside somewhere.
- Rising out of the gloom, impossibly, a keep of mismatched, scavenged materials -- a single visible entrance, and the husk rat is already bolting toward it with the key, giving the party a clear direction to chase rather than an open cavern to wander.
- The rat doesn't just enter the keep -- it beelines for one specific door deeper in (visible in the distance, standing open), vanishes through it, and the door **slams shut behind it** the instant it clears the threshold. No visible hand does it. This is the party's first hint that this particular door is Room 4 -- the boss room -- and that whatever's behind it can and will keep them out just as easily as it kept the rat in. It stays firmly shut (and unopenable by ordinary means) until the party has the key found in the false safe room.

#### The Husk Rat (stat block)

Hairless, hide gone stiff and grey, milky white eyes that never blink and never quite track right. It doesn't run so much as scuttle, stiff and twitchy, on too many limbs -- thin, finger-like legs sprouting wrong from its abdomen the way a spider's legs bud from its body, working the corpse of the rat like a puppet from underneath. Wrong in a way that takes a second too long to name.

#### Fentrik -- the survivor (courtyard RP/puzzle challenge)
- The party finds **Fentrik**, wounded and terrified, near the keep's entrance inside the courtyard's ward.
- **At a Glance**
	- **Trope/Reference:** The shell-shocked castaway who's been alone way too long -- think Tom Hanks in *Cast Away*, but worse.
	- **Voice:** Cracked, quiet, keeps trailing off mid-sentence like he forgets anyone's listening.
	- **Memorable Tidbit:** Flinches at sudden movement; keeps one hand pressed to his ear like it aches.
	- **Home:** A merchant sailor, swallowed by the dreadnought fairly recently (relatively -- time is strange in the donjon).
	- **Purpose:** Runs simultaneously with the courtyard puzzle -- the party has to split attention between treating/questioning him and solving the puzzle, before he transforms and the scene turns into a fight.
- **What actually happened to him (DM only):**
	- He dropped into the cavern outside the courtyard's ward -- the blight underfoot broke his fall, but also infected him. The infection wasn't immediate.
	- Early-stage and asymptomatic, he was able to carry the infection past the keep's own defenses (which would otherwise have kept the blight out) and fled inside.
	- The horrors within the keep drove him back out. On the way, he was wounded -- and his now-infected blood let the blight finally take hold inside the keep itself.
	- He fled into the courtyard, whose wards still hold against the blight -- it's the only reason he's lasted this long. He has no real understanding of any of this; he just knows the courtyard feels safe and the keep doesn't.
	- **He's holding back one thing:** he found a key somewhere inside the keep that would let the party in. He doesn't mention it -- he's too frightened of what's inside to want anyone going back in, himself included. A successful Insight check, or good roleplay pressure, can surface that he's hiding something before he transforms.
- **What he can tell the party before things go wrong (dole out as much or as little as needed):**
	- The keep above is old, and something lives in it that isn't undead in the usual sense -- "the walls bleed, and they remember."
	- He's heard a woman's voice singing/whispering from deep in the keep, and anyone who follows it doesn't come back the same.
	- Whatever's crawling in the dark outside avoids the keep itself now -- until very recently, that made the keep the safer bet.
- **The turn:** while the party treats him and/or works the courtyard puzzle, the infection finally takes hold. Black blood trickles, then pours, from his ear -- then tendrils burst out of his skull. The head tears free of the body and becomes **the Weeping Head** (below), interrupting the puzzle scene directly.

## The Main Hallway (corridor/gallery)
> The passage narrows, walls close enough to brush on both sides, every surface stained the color of old wine. Whatever this place is, it isn't rot -- it's something closer to scar tissue.

- Design intent: no monster is ever shown. A patron who drifts even briefly out of the party's direct line of sight (rounding a corner first, lagging to check something, being "the last one through a doorway") is simply gone when attention returns to them.
- No sound, no struggle, no blood spray -- just absence. Something small and personal is left behind exactly where they were standing (Old Maud's knitting basket, Tobin's untied boot, Garrek's lute case) as the only evidence anything happened at all.
- The walls themselves periodically "weep" -- thin trails of dark ichor seeping from hairline cracks, always after the fact, in the stretch of corridor where the disappearance happened. It's never present beforehand as a warning, only after, as confirmation.
- If pressed for an explanation (Arcana/Investigation/Religion), the best the party can get is that the corridor itself seems to absorb rather than attack -- consistent with "the walls bleed, and they remember" from Fentrik, but never resolved further here.

## Ashyra's Study
> A heavy door, unmarked and undamaged, opens onto a room untouched by the ruin outside -- shelves of water-stained books, a broad writing desk buried under loose papers, and in the corner, half-hidden behind a collapsed shelf, a nest of filthy bedding gone stiff with age. For the first time since the portal, it feels like nowhere is watching.

- Reads, and is meant to read, as genuinely defensible: single door, no visible corruption, no ambient dread cues -- a legitimate spot for the party to call a long rest.
- This was [[Ashyra Veil|Ashyra]]'s own study, and the nest of bedding in the corner is where she slept, centuries ago -- the room's quiet isn't a ward or accident, it's the residue of the one place she ever let her guard down. Something has since taken up the same habit, using the room's old stillness to rest between hunts exactly the way she once did.
- The party can spend downtime searching her papers. Each attempt takes **5 minutes** and can be repeated; roll Investigation each time -- her notes are scattered, not organized, so different attempts can turn up different fragments regardless of order. Read the result aloud in her voice: first-person, unraveling further with each deeper tier.
- **(Party found all of it):** the full set, cleaned up and reordered, is published as [[The Journals and Papers of Ashyra Veil]].

	- **DC 5 -- her name, and power without faith:**
	  > *"My name was Ashyra Veil, for whatever that's still worth to anyone. I have not knelt at an altar in longer than I can say, and yet I want, and yet I am answered. Let that trouble whichever god still bothers to look for me. I do not need to be chosen to be capable. I only ever needed to be clever enough to take what was never going to be given."*

	- **DC 10 -- the blight as her power source:**
	  > *"It is not an altar, and it is not a god, but it answers like one if I feed it enough. I bring it what the dreadnought drops -- meat, bone, whatever this place has finished with -- and it gives back more than I put in, the way a hearth gives back more warmth than the log you laid on it. I have stopped calling it a well. A well does not want. This wants. I have simply been careful never to give it enough to matter."*

	- **DC 15 -- the wards, the gate, and her last entry:**
	  > *"The wards around this study, and the ring outside it, are the same working as the chamber door -- lead and old law bent to the same purpose: keep the worst of what's out there from ever reaching in. The gate took longer. I built it in pieces, over more years than I let myself count, waiting each time for the dreadnought to swallow some new fool's forgotten cargo -- a hinge here, a keystone there -- until finally there was enough of the world's own metal and craft in it to open."*

	  > *[a final entry, in a hastier, uneven hand]*
	  > *"Something is wrong tonight. I feel it in my own blood now, not just in the offerings I bring it. I will not let it finish what it's started in me. Whatever comes next, I do not accept that this ends me. I have refused every god that ever tried to end me. I will refuse this too."*

	- **DC 20 -- Atropus, obscure and foreboding:**
	  > *"At the spell weaver ruins by the Great Rift, I found reference to a thing not yet born and already dead -- a stillbirth adrift somewhere out past the world's own light, dreaming of the moment it finally opens its eyes. They wrote of it the way one writes of weather, inevitable and far off, and did not seem to understand what they were describing. I understood. I have spent my whole life being hunted by gods who feared what I might become. I would give a great deal to see them feel, even once, what it is to be afraid of something that cannot be reasoned with or prayed to. Perhaps that thing, when it wakes, will do what I never could."*

	- **DC 25 -- her suspicion of Moander:**
	  > *"I have avoided writing the name for a long time, as though not naming it kept it from being true. It is not simply divine residue, not simply a fragment. I have seen what it does to the meat I feed it, how it doesn't rot so much as *continue*. I believe it is Moander, or what centuries left of him. I do not know if that changes anything I do here. I suspect it changes everything."*

	- Sitting upright on a shelf among the ruined books, unnervingly out of place, is a **Talking Doll** -- functional, watching, and in a room like this, deeply unwelcome to discover.
	- **Visible the moment the party enters, no search required:** scrawled in dried, rust-dark streaks across the bookshelves and the wall above the writing desk, in a hand nothing like her neat journal script:
  > *"Moon's haunted."*
  No context, no follow-up -- written in her own blood, at some point after everything else in this room was already written. Pure unease, the only trace of what she learned at the spell weaver ruins beyond what's in the DC 20 entry.
- **The night terror:** if the party sleeps here, one PC (DM's choice, excluding the party's Thri-Kreen, who doesn't sleep) wakes in the middle of the night to the sensation of something unseen pinning them down -- hands, or something like them, sliding up over their throat to cradle the skull, then wrenching their head up and slamming it back down against whatever they're sleeping on. No damage, no mark, no visible cause. They simply wake again a moment later, shaken, certain it happened and unable to prove it did.
	- The Thri-Kreen doesn't sleep, and is effectively always the one keeping watch. From across the room, all it actually sees is the sleeper's whole body lurch forward as if yanked upright, then slam back down flat -- fast, silent, over in an instant. No visible cause, nothing else in the room moves. It saw *something* happen, but can't explain what caused it, and can't corroborate whatever the sleeper swears they felt.
	- **The key:** the PC who experienced the night terror wakes to find a key clenched in their hand that wasn't there before -- distinct from the stolen portal key, this one opens another lead-lined room elsewhere in the keep that the party has otherwise had no way into. (This is the boss room -- see below/Room 4.) No explanation given for how it got there; it simply is there now.
- On waking from the rest, no signs of forced entry or struggle -- a patron is either simply gone (bedroll empty, undisturbed) or found changed/wrong in a small, specific way that reads as impossible to have happened without anyone noticing (should have required waking the whole room to do quietly).
- Play the rest completely straight -- the reveal only lands if the players genuinely believed they were safe.

## Storage Room
> A voice -- unmistakably one of your own -- calls out from a side passage, thin and afraid. In the other direction, a half-open door reveals a long storeroom, shelves and crates stacked floor to ceiling with salvage -- coils of rope, bent tools, mismatched armor pieces, jars of things better not examined closely, all of it sorted with a care nothing else in this keep has shown. One crate's lid is already pried loose, coins visible inside.

- This was [[Ashyra Veil|Ashyra]]'s salvage store -- everything she judged worth keeping from centuries of the dreadnought's leavings, catalogued and shelved against some future use that never came. Unlike the rest of the keep's dishevelment, this room is organized, deliberate, almost obsessively tidy -- a quiet, unsettling contrast that says whoever kept it was still, in her own way, entirely lucid about at least this much.
- Most of it is exactly what it looks like: junk, curiosities, and dead ends -- present to make the one real prize (the Bag of Holding below) harder to distinguish from clutter at a glance, and to make clear this was a hoarder's room, not a treasury.
- Two lures presented at once, splitting the party's attention and the patrons' impulses:
	- **The voice:** something mimics a missing (or momentarily out-of-sight) patron's voice calling for help from one direction -- pulling searchers away from where that patron actually is (or already isn't).
	- **The reward:** the crates in the half-open room hold a genuine prize -- a **Bag of Holding**, sitting in plain sight in the nearest open crate -- tuned to catch whichever patron is most impulsive or eager to help/prove themselves (Tobin's eagerness, Renn's greed).
	- A patron who follows either lure alone is the one who doesn't come back -- punished for kindness or carelessness rather than a combat mistake, so it reads as misfortune rather than a monster ambush.
- Nothing here should ever be confronted directly -- if the party investigates immediately, they find only the aftermath (an already-fading voice, an already-empty room), never a creature to fight.

## The Boss Room
> The key turns, and the door the rat vanished through swings open onto a chamber choking with the blight -- thick, wet, crawling up every surface, breathing in a slow tidal rhythm that has nothing to do with any wind. Nothing about this room looks like it wants visitors.

> The rat, still clutching the stolen portal key, suddenly freezes and starts to cower -- then convulses, its silhouette bulging wrong in the dim light. Its spine arches until something audibly gives; skin splits along its back in a wet tear, and rather than blood, thick ropes of pale, wriggling tissue spill out and immediately begin to *grow*, doubling and redoubling in mass in seconds. The rat's own skull distends and cracks open like an egg from the inside as a second mass tears free of the first with a sound like a wet cloth ripping -- two roughly spherical, glistening bodies now, each already sprouting a ring of mismatched eyes and mouths that open and start gibbering nonsense before the transformation has even finished. What's left of the original rat is indistinguishable in the mess.

- **The door slams and the room seals:** the birth itself does it -- a wave of pressure and loosed ichor rolls out from the two new mouthers as they tear free of each other, slamming the chamber door shut behind the party (DC 20 Athletics/Acrobatics to pull free if still in the doorway) and hauling anyone lingering near the threshold bodily back inside on a lash of wet, half-formed tendrils that retract into the mouthers themselves the instant the door seals. No one gets to simply hang back and watch.

#### The Gibbering Mouthers (stat block)
> What's left of the husk rat is two roughly spherical masses now, glistening and wet, each ringed with mismatched eyes that never blink in unison and mouths that never stop moving -- muttering, giggling, chewing at the air -- like something tried to build a chorus out of parts that were never meant to speak.

**Medium aberration**, chaotic neutral
**HP** 52
**AC** 9 | 
**Speed** 20 ft., swim 20 ft.
**STR** 10 (+0) **DEX** 8 (-1) **CON** 16 (+3) **INT** 3 (-4) **WIS** 10 (+0) **CHA** 6 (-2)
**Condition Immunities:** prone
**Senses:** darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception 10
- **Aberrant Ground:** the ground in a 10-foot emanation from the mouther is difficult terrain.
- **Gibbering:** the mouther babbles incoherently while it doesn't have the Incapacitated condition. Any creature that starts its turn within 20 ft. of it while it's babbling must succeed on a DC 10 Wisdom save or roll 1d8: (1-4) does nothing; (5-6) uses no action or bonus action and all its movement to move in a random direction; (7-8) makes a melee attack against a randomly determined creature within reach, or does nothing if it can't.
- **Bite:** +2 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. *Hit:* 7 (2d6) piercing damage, and if the target is Medium or smaller, it falls prone. A creature reduced to 0 HP by this attack is absorbed, leaving only its equipment behind.
- **Blinding Spittle (Recharge 5-6):** each creature in a 10-foot-radius sphere within 30 ft. must succeed on a DC 10 Dexterity save or take 7 (2d6) radiant damage and be blinded until the end of the mouther's next turn.


### Phase 1 -- The Eye Opens
The wall stays just a wall -- more blight-crusted stone, nothing more -- until the first gibbering mouther dies. Read the following once that happens:

  > *For a moment, nothing -- then the room's breathing catches, like something inhaling sharply through its teeth. One by one, the wall gives back what it swallowed. Old Maud's knitting needles push through first, followed by Maud herself, dragged out of the stone waist-first. Renn Vask comes next, and he's still grinning, like some part of him hasn't caught up to what's happening yet.*
  >
  > *They don't speak. They don't seem to see you. Their throats open at once, and one note comes out of them together -- pitched too high, too perfectly matched, like a choir that was never meant to sing in unison finding the same wrong note anyway. It builds. It doesn't stop building. And then, all at once, each of them comes apart -- not torn, not cut, just* stops holding together, *skin and meat letting go in the same instant, four bodies collapsing into a single wet red spray that hits the floor and doesn't spread so much as vanish, soaked down into the stone like the room was thirsty.*
  >
  > *The wall where they were pulled from is already changing. Cracks spider outward from a single point at chest height -- not a whole face, not yet, just this: one eye, vast, forcing its way out of the stone on a stalk of wet, rotted sinew. It hangs dead and clouded, filmed over like a corpse three days gone. Beside it, a second socket splits open in the rock -- empty, waiting -- and inside it, something sick and green begins, slowly, to glow.*

- **The warning:** [[Azhura]]'s, [[Mantis]]'s, and [[Quinsley]]'s amulets all tingle the moment the eye opens -- an intense, wordless certainty that this thing is utterly beyond what any of them can fight, and that the only correct move is to get out.
	- [[Quinsley]], as a cleric, feels something the amulet alone doesn't explain: her own divine sense catches the fluttering, unmistakable spark of a dark divinity buried somewhere behind that eye -- faint, wounded, but real. Not just a monster to her; genuinely something god-touched, however broken.

- **The first strike:** the instant the second socket finishes glowing, motes of darkness gather in front of it, then blast out at Tok Tok -- obliterating half of his upper body, dropping him to below 0 hp without outright killing him (8d6 necrotic damage). Read the following once it lands:

  > *The eye doesn't so much fire as exhale, and Tok Tok simply stops being whole. Something hot and wet slaps across your faces and hands all at once -- and for one long, ringing second, you'd swear it was his blood, because what else could it be. Then you feel the sting of it. Then you see it isn't red -- it's pale, and fibrous, and studded through with hard little splinters that are working themselves out of your skin even as you notice them, the way a spray of shattered wood would. Somewhere underneath the wet is old, dry sap, sharp as a fresh-cut plank. Tok Tok is on the ground, what's left of him, and where the wound should be raw meat and bone there is instead a ruin of pale grain and pulp and something dark and viscous threaded through it like sap through a living tree -- unmistakably a body, unmistakably not only that. Whatever he is, he has never once told you, and now you're **wearing** the proof of it.*

- **What Phase 1 actually is:** just the eye -- no body, no tentacles, nothing to melee but a chunk of wall. Its only job right now is to sit there and threaten the beam again in four rounds while the surviving gibbering mouther keeps the party busy up close. See *The Face of Moander* stat block below.

### Phase 2 -- The Face Emerges
The moment the face's HP (one pool, tracked across both phases) drops to 67 or below -- half its total -- read the following:

  > *The glow behind the eye guts out, the socket collapsing back into the stone like a held breath finally let go. For a moment the wall is just a wall again -- and then the floor in front of it splits instead, a long, wet tear opening in the ground as something far larger than the eye ever was drags itself up and out. It isn't stone. It's a face -- vast, rotted down past any expression a face should still be able to make, tendrils sliding wetly free of a mouth big enough to walk into, hauling the rest of itself up out of the tear until it looms over the room from ground level rather than from the wall behind it. It opens that mouth, and doesn't scream so much as birth -- two ropy, man-shaped tangles of dark vine hit the floor wet on either side of it, unknotting themselves upright.*

- **Vine Blights:** 2 spill out with the face's emergence -- see stat block below.
- **Tentacles:** the face can now reach into the room directly; it gains **Multiattack** and the **Rotting Tentacle** action (see stat block).
- **The eye beam keeps ticking:** Phase 2 doesn't reset the countdown -- it's still the same eye, still the same clock, just relocated. Track it continuously across both phases.
- **The actual objective:** fight through (or past) the remaining gibbering mouther and the vine blights, force the door back open, and get out of the room -- the win condition is escape, not victory over the face itself.

#### The Vine Blights (stat block)
> Two ropy shapes hit the floor wet on either side of the newly emerged face -- man-shaped tangles of dark, glistening vine, thorned and dripping, that unknot themselves upright with a sound like wet rope pulled taut.

**Medium plant**, neutral evil
**HP** 19
**AC** 12 (natural armor) | 
**Speed** 20 ft.
**STR** 15 (+2) **DEX** 8 (-1) **CON** 14 (+2) **INT** 5 (-3) **WIS** 10 (+0) **CHA** 3 (-4)
**Senses:** blindsight 60 ft., passive Perception 10
- **Constricting Vine:** +4 to hit, reach 10 ft., one target. *Hit:* 6 (1d8 + 2) bludgeoning damage. On a hit against a Large or smaller creature, the target is **grappled** (escape DC 12); while grappled this way, it takes 4 (1d8) bludgeoning damage at the start of each of its turns, and the blight can't use Constricting Vine again while it holds them.
- **Entangling Plants (Recharge 5-6):** casts *Entangle* (Constitution spellcasting, save DC 12).

#### The Face of Moander (two-phase stat block)
> Phase 1 is a single eye forced out of the wall on a stalk of rotted sinew -- no body, no reach, just a countdown. Phase 2 is what's underneath: the whole face, hauled up out of the ground in front of the wall once it's been hurt enough to stop hiding.

**Gargantuan aberration**, chaotic evil
**HP** 135 (10d20 + 30) -- one pool across both phases; Phase 2 triggers the instant it drops to 67 or below
**AC** 15 in Phase 1 (a small, half-embedded target), 14 in Phase 2 (the full face is a much easier target)
**Speed** 0 ft. (see **Rooted**)
**STR** 16 (+3) **DEX** 8 (-1) **CON** 16 (+3) **INT** 6 (-2) **WIS** 14 (+2) **CHA** 15 (+2)
**Saves:** Con +6, Wis +5
**Damage Vulnerabilities:** fire, radiant
**Damage Immunities:** poison
**Condition Immunities:** charmed, frightened, grappled, paralyzed, petrified, poisoned, prone, restrained
**Senses:** truesight 60 ft. (it *is* the room -- nothing in it goes unseen), passive Perception 12
**Languages:** understands Common and Deep Speech, but only ever gibbers
**Proficiency Bonus:** +3
**CR** 5 (1,800 XP)
###### Traits
- **Rooted:** can't be moved, knocked prone, or forced from the wall or ground by any means short of destroying the structure itself.
- **Legendary Resistance (1/Day):** if it fails a saving throw, it can choose to succeed instead.
- **Rotted Core:** this is an unfinished fragment of Moander, and the rot holding it together is exactly as flammable and light-averse as the rest of the god's blight. It takes double damage from fire and radiant sources -- the one real lever a level 3 party has to actually kill it rather than just survive it. Nothing in the room signals this outright; it's meant to be discovered in play (a torch, a cleric's radiant spell, a lucky *burning hands*) and then remembered.
- **Aberrant Corruption (Phase 2 only):** once the full face has emerged, the ground in a 15-foot emanation around it is difficult terrain (as the gibbering mouthers' Aberrant Ground).
###### Actions
- **Eye Beam (both phases).** +6 ranged spell attack, range 120 ft., one target. *Hit:* 28 (8d6) necrotic damage. This is the only action available in Phase 1. Recharges on a fixed 4-round cycle rather than a die roll -- see **The eye's recharge** below.
- **Multiattack (Phase 2 only):** makes two **Rotting Tentacle** attacks.
- **Rotting Tentacle (Phase 2 only).** +6 to hit, reach 20 ft., one target. *Hit:* 10 (2d6 + 3) bludgeoning damage plus 3 (1d6) necrotic damage, and the target is **grappled** (escape DC 13). While grappled this way, the tentacle can instead drag the target up to 15 ft. toward the mouth on a later hit instead of dealing damage.
###### Special
- **Phase Shift (once, at 50% HP):** the eye sinks back into the wall and the full face tears free of the ground in front of it -- see *Phase 2 -- The Face Emerges* above. This doesn't cost the face its turn; resolve it the instant its HP crosses the threshold, even mid-round.
- **The eye's recharge (already fired once):** the opening strike against **[[Tok Tok]]** above *is* this attack's first use -- treat it as already expended when the fight formally begins. It refires automatically once **4 full rounds** have passed since its last use, in either phase; this is a fixed countdown, not a random recharge, so track it openly or on a visible cue (the eye's glow visibly building) so the players can feel the clock. If the party hasn't escaped by the time it fires again, it targets **[[Tok Tok]]** first if he's still down and present -- badly maiming rather than outright killing him if you want him survivable, otherwise the nearest PC.

**(DM Only) Running this fight:** Phase 1 is a pure pressure clock -- nothing to melee but a chunk of wall, so let that stretch be about the surviving gibbering mouther and the party deciding whether to commit to the fight or the door. Phase 2 is where the real damage race starts: two more bodies (the vine blights) plus tentacles plus the same beam clock, all at once, right as the party's resources are already dented. The overall CR 5 rating is an average across the whole fight -- Phase 1 alone plays closer to CR 3-4 (nothing but a recharge attack), Phase 2 alone closer to CR 6-7 (full Multiattack plus two adds); together across a full encounter they should land around CR 5. **Rotted Core** (fire/radiant vulnerability) applies in both phases and is the real lever for actually finishing it rather than just surviving it.

- **The actual objective:** fight through (or past) the remaining gibbering mouther and the vine blights, force the door back open, and get out of the room -- the win condition is escape, not victory over the face itself.

## Room 5 -- the flight back to the portal
> The whole keep groans as you run -- and the walls themselves seem to notice you leaving. Wet, ropy shapes tear free of the plaster ahead of you, grasping.

- The fragment of Moander in the boss room doesn't want to let them go. From the moment the party breaks for the door, the entire keep's fleshy, blight-soaked interior becomes actively hostile for the length of the retreat back through Room 3, the Courtyard, and into the Sealed Chamber.
- **Blight Tendrils (environmental hazard, not a creature):**
	- Each round of the retreat, one or more tendrils burst from the nearest wall, floor, or ceiling near a party member and lash out. +5 to hit, reach 10 ft.; on a hit, the target takes 1d4 necrotic damage and is **grappled** (escape DC 10).
	- Low danger, high nuisance: the damage is minor and the grapple is easy to break, but it costs an action or slows the party down, exactly the friction you want during a chase.
	- **The tendrils cannot meaningfully be killed.** They have no practical HP for this purpose -- sever one and another simply erupts elsewhere a moment later. The correct play is always to break free and keep moving, never to fight back.
	- Escalate or de-escalate the frequency/number of simultaneous tendrils freely based on how the table's doing -- this is pure pacing pressure for the run back, not a balanced combat encounter.
- Once the party (and the portal key/holy symbol) are back in the Sealed Chamber and the door is shut behind them, the tendrils don't follow -- the lead lining holds against this the same way it always has. The gate can then be reactivated to end the session.

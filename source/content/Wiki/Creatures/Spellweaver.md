Spellweavers (properly "Spell Weavers," sometimes miswritten as one word) are a strange, highly intelligent humanoid species native to another Prime Material plane entirely. They once ruled a multiverse-spanning empire built on an unmatched command of arcane magic, before that empire collapsed in a single catastrophic event.

## Description
Spellweavers are frail, alien-looking humanoids: gray-skinned (occasionally mottled with beige, pink, or yellow), hairless, roughly five feet tall, with six arms, a long flexible neck, large dark eyes, and blood that runs metallic blue. Younger individuals have darker skin that lightens with age.

### Abilities
Their minds are so deeply attuned to magic that they can cast several spells simultaneously, cast without verbal or material components, sense magic and invisibility at range, and communicate telepathically. Their alien psychology makes them immune to mind-affecting effects and to scrying, and anyone foolish enough to try to reach them telepathically suffers a painful backlash. Some can also generate a temporary field that blocks scrying and teleportation around themselves.

### Chromatic disks
Every spellweaver carries a palm-sized disk grown from its own body during meditation — a nearly indestructible lump of resin that constantly cycles through colors, a trait that gives the disks their name. The color-shifting is deliberate and serves as a form of communication between spellweavers. Functionally, the disks are enormously powerful magical batteries, and can also be used to imprison the souls of powerful spellcasters. They explode if anyone other than their owner tries to use them.

### Lifecycle and reproduction
A spellweaver's natural lifespan is about six centuries. When one senses its end approaching, it enters a long regenerative trance — consuming vast quantities of magic items in the process — emerging rejuvenated with another six centuries of life. This can happen up to six times. On the sixth and final renewal, the elder instead performs a self-sacrificing ritual that consumes its body entirely and produces six new adult spellweavers who inherit its memories and abilities. Both rituals are secrets the species has never shared with outsiders.

## Personality
Spellweavers consider their civilization older than the gods themselves, and see themselves as above divinity entirely, holding no interest in divine magic. They are relentless collectors of magic items — needed to fuel their rituals and machinery — and while willing to trade for what they want, they have no qualms about taking it by force or manipulation instead.

## Activities
Spellweavers are credited with creating the first black obelisks, artifacts capable of manipulating time itself — knowledge later stolen from them by the archmage Vecna.

## Combat
When spellweavers fight, it's rarely spontaneous: they plan raids months in advance using scrying and spies, then execute them with teleportation and invisibility, disabling guards just long enough to seize their target — usually a coveted magic item or the soul of a powerful spellcaster — before teleporting away again.

## Society
Sages divide spellweavers into three loose specialties: Battle Weavers, deadly six-armed melee duelists who hunt worthy souls to claim; Thought Weavers, psychic masterminds who plan raids and long-term schemes; and Hex Weavers, who draw power from the Shadowfell and often pursue agendas even other spellweavers find inscrutable. Since their empire's fall, spellweavers rarely cooperate except in temporary raiding parties formed to acquire a specific item.

### Goals
Much of a spellweaver's existence is spent trying to rebuild what was lost — seeking out the ruins of their old colonies, called nodes, massive stone-and-steel structures that once housed soul- and magic-draining furnaces linked across the planes by portals. Intact furnaces no longer exist, but spellweavers keep searching, since a working one would rival the power of a god. They also hunt magical gems that hold fragments of the "Code of Reversion" — a spell that, if ever fully reassembled, could reset the entire multiverse to the moment before their empire fell.

### Lairs
Where they can't find or rebuild a node, spellweavers sometimes establish smaller lairs marked by clusters of rune-carved stone or wood pillars, whose true purpose remains unknown — those who study them too closely tend to suffer for it, from headaches to lasting mental harm.

### Religion
True to their disdain for gods, spellweavers worship nothing and practice no religion, though they hold something like reverence for their own ancestors, often meditating on the golden age of their empire. The number six carries deep significance for them, echoed in their lifespan, their reproductive cycle, and even their base-six counting system.

### Language
Spellweavers possess an intricate spoken language of clicks and whistles, but an ancient taboo forbids its use, meant to sharpen their telepathic gifts instead — even in pain, they only ever grunt or shriek. All that survives of their spoken tongue is its written form. In practice they layer three channels of communication at once — telepathy (reaching up to a thousand miles), the shifting color of their disks, and a complex sign language using their arms and neck — allowing them to convey enormous amounts of information in very little time, in a way wholly incomprehensible to outsiders. Oddly, they sometimes leave behind written notes for other races to find, though these are invariably cryptic and unhelpful.

### The old empire
At its height the spellweaver empire was largely peaceful, acting as a kind of detached steward over the multiverse, occasionally pressing lesser races into labor. Above all, they were scholars — traveling the planes to study other cultures' languages and symbols, a pursuit that fed their telepathic and magical mastery. Any race they judged a genuine threat, they would quietly arm with dangerous magic and let destroy itself from within — a tactic some surviving spellweavers still practice.

## Uses
Spellweaver remains retain magical properties; a spellweaver skull was reportedly one ingredient in a cure devised against the zombie plague that struck Chult in the late 1400s DR.

## Relationships
Spellweavers share a close bond with the nerra, mirror-skinned humanoids who help construct and maintain their portal networks — some suspect the nerra were the spellweavers' own creation. They aren't above employing other races as servants or forming alliances of convenience, favoring those hostile to the gods, such as efreet; when they need to negotiate, they favor the City of Brass as neutral ground.

## History
By the spellweavers' own account, their empire predates the gods and primordials and once spanned the multiverse. It met its end resisting an incursion of chaos from the Far Realm: the empire held, mostly, but the intrusion still ruptured its homeland, scattering the wreckage across the planes and — by their telling — giving rise to the Astral Sea and the Elemental Chaos in the process. Spellweavers call this catastrophe the Disjunction, the age before it the Time of Nodes, and the age after the Scrabbling.

Sages are skeptical of that account. Some think the empire actually reached for total mastery over all communication and paid for its ambition; others believe it attempted a doomed working to restore a once-unified reality, and that its furnaces failed in a single cascading chain reaction, destroying almost everyone at once. Only the few spellweavers who happened to be away from the nodes survived to found the modern remnant of the race.

On Toril, spellweaver activity has surfaced more than once: their presence beneath the city of Treshla drew a beholder attack that destroyed the city above in 1101 DR, driving the local colony deeper underground into the ruins of Barakuir (rumors persist of survivors still lurking beneath Treshla). In 1374 DR, a colony allied with the gold dwarves of the Great Rift against duergar and beholder forces during the war to reclaim Shanatar.

### Legacy
The spellweavers' black obelisk technology outlived their empire — Netherese arcanists eventually recovered its secrets and built smaller versions to power their flying enclaves, scattering the devices across Faerûn when those cities eventually fell.

## Rumors & legends
Some claim both spellweavers and thri-kreen share a hidden connection to Jergal, the god who was once Lord of the End of Everything. The true cause of the Disjunction remains debated even among spellweavers themselves — theories range from a forbidden bid at godhood punished by the gods, to a failed attempt to reunite a once-single reality, to a darker rumor that Vecna used the obelisks' secrets to erase the spellweavers from history outright (a claim hard to square with confirmed sightings of the race as late as the 1300s DR). Whatever their present goal, some sages suspect the survivors are still quietly working to force the planes back together — an act that, if it succeeded, would likely kill everything, gods included.

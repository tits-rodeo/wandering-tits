## (DM Only) The Dark of It

### The Spell Weavers
In the Realms, there existed a [[Spellweaver]] colony, known as [[Eril]], in the northern reaches of Faerûn at the base of a cliff on the northwestern shores of the Narrow Sea (Modern: [[Ascore]]) with ready access to a nearby gem mine. From this redoubt, the spell
weavers observed the empires of elves and dwarves, attended by a tribe of primitive humans, who they bred, selecting for their potential mastery of the Art.

In -3939 DR, the Disjunction destroyed Faerûn’s spell weaver Node in a great conflagration, wiping out every spell weaver save one, a spell weaver named Jergal who was being birthed at the time. Although he possessed the entirety of his parent’s memories, Jergal found himself alone in this new world: all his fellow spell
weavers were dead and the primitive humans they had long kept as servitors and bred for their potential in the Art had fled down the shores of the Narrow Sea. And yet, Jergal found within himself a spark of divinity, suggesting that at least in some way, the Ascension had succeeded.

### The Code of Reversion
As had been long planned by the spell weaver race, Jergal set himself on a course to rebuild the spell weaver empire by reassembling the Code of Reversion, psychometrically inscribed in precious gemstones by the spell weavers before the Disjunction as a safety measure to allow them to repeat the experiment if something went wrong. The Code of Reversion was designed to allow a single spell weaver to cast a multi-part spell to reverse time in the entire multiverse to just before the Disjunction and then improve upon the experiment that attempted the mass divine ascension of the race. Unfortunately, Jergal realized the devastation caused by the first attempt was so great that the gemstones in which the Code of Reversion was inscribed were scattered across the multiverse in the conflagration.

Alone and bereft of allies, Jergal began his monumental task of restoring the Time of Nodes. Given the divine spark within him and the loss of the Code of Reversion, he decided to create a cult of worshipers and use them to create an army of undead. The undead
—undying, able to operate in any environment without sustenance, and controllable—would then be able to scour the multiverse for the lost lore needed to reverse time.

### A Failed Cult Gives Birth to Netheril
Jergal began by nurturing a cult of worshipers led by shamans among the descendants of the spell weavers’ former human servitors, who dwelled in small fishing villages on the shore of the Narrow Sea. Many of these humans were receptive to venerating Jergal as the Lord of the End of Everything (a title that ironically referred to the Code of Reversion), given their long cultural heritage of serving spell weaver masters. As the cult’s worship nurtured Jergal’s divine spark, shamans of Jergal rose to power in each village thanks to their power over life and death and their mastery of necromancy. Jergal then began inciting (through telepathic whispers) the orc tribes of the region to attack his followers. As he had planned, the human villages came together in mutual defense in -3859 DR against the orcs of the Horde Unending to form the Alliance of Seventon under the leadership of a shaman-king, Nether the Elder. As he grew in power, the shaman-king renamed the fledgling kingdom “Netheril,” which meant “Nether’s Land.”

Once Netheril was established, Jergal’s shamans began to teach promising Netherese the Art of necromancy and turn their energy towards creating a vast army of undead. However, the machinations of the Lord of the End of Everything did not go unnoticed, and the history of Netheril began to evolve beyond Jergal’s control due to the influence of outside powers: the Fair Folk of Eaerlann and the lich-king of Oreme.

With his original plan to recover the Code of Reversion in ruins, Jergal turned away from direct manipulation of the Netherese and embraced his divinity as their Lord of the Dead. While Jergal did not abandon his efforts to recover the Code of Reversion, such efforts moved beyond the ken of mortals and took centuries to unfold. However, the seeds Jergal had sown as a spell weaver, coupled with the separate machinations of the Fair Folk and Issarnathass, led the Netherese to amazing heights of magic. Even the legendary mythallar of the Netherese owe their inspiration to the gigantic magical furnaces of spell weaver Nodes

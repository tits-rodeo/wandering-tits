Ascore was once the great port city of Delzoun, the dwarven realm of the North, standing on the shores of the Narrow Sea as the gateway between the dwarves and the outside world. Today it's a wind-scoured ruin half-buried in the sands of Anauroch, near the Frozen Sea, and rumored to be watched over by something far worse than the monsters that already prowl its bones.

## Description
In its prime, Ascore was a wealthy trade hub where Delzoun's dwarves dealt with the elves of Eaerlann and Myth Drannor and the humans of Netheril and Nimbral. The dwarves built their docks -- and their ships -- entirely of stone, on a massive scale: up to twenty of these stone ships could sit in harbor at once, with thirty more at sea, plying the Narrow Sea for trade and war alike.

As the Narrow Sea receded, the dwarves abandoned the city, and the desert swallowed it. What's left is a lonely, desolate ruin: stone docks jutting from dunes that used to be open water, and the huge, empty hulks of the stone ships still emerging from the sand to the east, the last remnant of Delzoun's navy.

## Trade
Ascore was Delzoun's window to the world. Goods from Netheril flowed in through its stone docks or along the Lowroad, a fortified subterranean highway connecting the two realms through the safest stretches of the Underdark. Ascore's stone ships carried trade across the Bay of Ascore and the Narrow Sea to Coldfoot, Harborage, and Zenith, and the city received shipments of gold and silver from the Netherese mining settlement of Bandor Village.

## Locations
### The Great Door
A road over a thousand years old runs west into a hill, ending at a massive door flanked by two crouching stone griffons. Beyond lies a black passage descending into the earth, which eventually surfaces again amid the ruins clustered at the base of the cliff above Ascore proper.

### The Stone Ships
Dozens of the dwarves' stone-hulled ships remain half-buried in the dunes east of the city, towering wrecks that once made up the whole of Delzoun's navy.

### The Red Pyramids
Thirteen tall, five-sided pyramids of a strange red stone stand in a ring at the city's center -- the same material, oddly, as the bleeding stone found in the ruins of Karse. Adventurers who've gotten close report the pyramids being used by a coven of hags as a focus for rituals, seemingly in worship of something ancient and terrible said to dwell beneath the city. Since the return of the City of Shade in 1372 DR, a pool of liquid shadow has pooled at the center of the ring, and nobody's quite sure why.

### The Hlaungadath Portal
A ruined chamber in Ascore holds the far end of a lost portal to the city of Hlaungadath. A thirty-line dwarven chant, still legible on a surviving wall and ending with the line "that the halls of our fathers may stand for 10,000 years," is said to activate it.

## Inhabitants
Orcs give Ascore a wide berth, apparently repelled by something worse than themselves. Undead are common throughout the ruins, said to answer to a coven of hags who've made the place their own. From 1372 DR a Shadovar expedition, led by an archmage, camped among the red pyramids for years, studying them and driving off anyone who got too curious. By the late 15th century DR, the ancient blue dragon Iymrith had claimed Ascore as part of her domain -- though she rarely visits in person, two of her offspring lair in a pair of the abandoned stone ships, and gargoyles roost throughout the ruins.

## History
The Netherese first opened trade with the Delzoun dwarves at Ascore around -3419 DR, striking a formal agreement three years later and building the Lowroad to connect the two peoples. After Netheril's collapse in -339 DR, Netherese refugees fleeing the fall of Runlatha crossed through Ascore and the Lowroad on their long migration west, aided by dwarven rangers from the city.

As the Narrow Sea dwindled, the dwarves began withdrawing from Ascore around -333 DR, gradually ceding the port to the encroaching desert. By 1370 DR, dwarven scouts from Sundabar reported a rising evil in the ruins and increasing numbers of monsters pouring out of the Underdark from Ascore's direction -- warnings that went largely unheeded until the Shadovar arrived two years later to begin their own study of the red pyramids.

## Rumors & Legends
Even now, tales persist that Ascore hides great treasure alongside whatever evil has lingered beneath it for two thousand years -- something enormous, ancient, and wicked, said to sleep somewhere far under the ruined city. No one has ever confirmed what the red pyramids were actually for, though some scholars link them to a similar ring of thirteen dire oaks found in the ruins of Karse.

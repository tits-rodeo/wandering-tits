Atropus, the World Born Dead, is an undead entity the size of a small planet — some say a primordial that died before it was ever truly alive. Its arrival anywhere is an omen: a harbinger of mass death, spreading undeath, and the ruin of entire worlds.

## (DM Only) The Dark of It
**Atropus**, also called **The World Born Dead**, is a massive being of unfathomable power. No one knows exactly how it came to be, but all agree that it has been around since Ao, the creator god of the Faerûnian pantheon, created the universe. Some claim that he is a Primordial, the race of deities that contested with the gods for rule of Abeir-Toril. Others say he is the last god created by Ao, a failed experiment resulting in a stillborn god. No matter what he is, it is known that he seeks to end life, and that his arrival is heralded by an increase in necromantic activity, leading many to believe he is the remnants of a god of death.

### Description
Atropus is both a place and a being at once: a planetoid roughly 700 miles across, its surface a barren expanse of black, pitted stone slicked with foul black slime, crawling with undead of every kind. Seen from the right angle, the whole moonlet resembles a vast, hairless skull, its glassy "eyes" alone spanning some 40 miles.

Atropus also manifests a focal aspect apart from its planetary bulk: a headless, roughly humanoid shape of fossilized flesh, coated in the same reeking slime that covers the rest of it.

### History
No sage agrees on where Atropus came from, and there may be a reason no single answer has ever held together.

Its first true appearance predates recorded history, tied by those who have gone looking to the First Sundering, circa -17,600 DR, the same elven working that split Merrouroboros and birthed Evermeet. That ritual's magic is known to stretch both backward and forward through time. Atropus carries the same signature: a Weave-scale catastrophe with exactly one thread that should have lived, and didn't.

For millennia after, it drifted through Realmspace. Around -11,000 DR it finally arrived at Glyth, drawn by the mass death spreading there as gith purges hunted the shattered illithid empire's survivors nearly to extinction. What happened when it arrived is not yet settled, even among those who study it closely. What is known is that the illithids of Glyth fled soon after, founding Oryndoll on Toril, and the planet sat unclaimed for millennia until they finally reestablished their domain there around 1267 DR.

Atropus touched the Realms again far more recently, and far more legibly. Its presence lines up with the Disjunction in -3939 DR, the catastrophic failure of the spell weavers' bid for godhood. [[Jergal]] was the sole survivor, mid-birth in his own parent's ritual when the backlash hit, meant to emerge as one of six. Sages who have pieced together this much suspect that some fragment of what should have been shared with him instead became Atropus, a stillborn twin thrown loose by the same working that gave Jergal his divine spark. If Jergal knows this, he has told no one still living.

What the ritual actually did, by the reckoning of those few who have pieced it together, was draw more Positive Energy than any working of its scale should have found in one place. It reached across ordinary space, and across time as well, borrowing from ages long past and ages not yet come, and it did not confine itself to Toril alone. Every place that draw touched, across countless realities and crystal spheres far beyond Realmspace, was left with a wound where Positive Energy should have been. Atropus is what rushed in to fill it: Negative Energy given form and hunger, born wherever the ritual's reach happened to leave the deepest absence. Jergal received the spark meant for six. Atropus is what the ritual took to pay for it.

Its third and most recent touch on the Realms came during the Second Sundering (1482 to 1489 DR), when Ao's rewriting of the Tablets of Fate rippled through reality once more. The handful of sages who track Atropus at all fear this is no coincidence, that whatever anchors it to those two great tears in Toril's history means its presence isn't settling so much as returning. As of 1493 DR, its attention has drifted well past Glyth, last reckoned somewhere beyond H'Catha at the edge of Realmspace. Its exact course, and what drew it there, remains unknown.

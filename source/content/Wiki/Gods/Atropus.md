Atropus, the World Born Dead, is an undead entity the size of a small planet — some say a primordial that died before it was ever truly alive. Its arrival anywhere is an omen: a harbinger of mass death, spreading undeath, and the ruin of entire worlds.

## (DM Only) The Dark of It
**Atropus**, also called **The World born Dead**, is a massive being of unfathomable power. No one knows exactly how it came to be, but all agree that it has been around since Ao, the creator god of the Faerûnian pantheon, created the universe. Some claim that he is a Primordial, the race of deities that contested with the gods for rule of Abeir-Toril. Others say he is the last god created by Ao, a failed experiment resulting in a stillborn god. No matter what he is, it is known that he seeks to end life, and that his arrival is heralded by an increase in necromantic activity, leading many to believe he is the remnants of a god of death.

### Description
Atropus is both a place and a being at once: a planetoid roughly 700 miles across, its surface a barren expanse of black, pitted stone slicked with foul black slime, crawling with undead of every kind. Seen from the right angle, the whole moonlet resembles a vast, hairless skull, its glassy "eyes" alone spanning some 40 miles.

Atropus also manifests a focal aspect apart from its planetary bulk: a headless, roughly humanoid shape of fossilized flesh, coated in the same reeking slime that covers the rest of it.

### History
No sage agrees on where Atropus came from. Some claim it is the rotted afterbirth left over when Ao first birthed the gods; others say it was itself one of those gods — the last one made, arriving already dead. Still others insist it is simply a primordial, corrupted into undeath by some unknown catastrophe.

Atropus was first seen orbiting the planet Glyth, drawn there by the wholesale slaughter the mind flayers of that world inflicted on their neighbors. It descended, and left Glyth a dead husk.

A handful of sages who know of Atropus at all fear its attention has since drifted toward Toril, and watch for the signs said to precede it: a sudden surge in necromantic power, and the dead rising without anyone having called them.

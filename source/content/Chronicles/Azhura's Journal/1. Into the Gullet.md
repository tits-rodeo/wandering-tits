## 12 Eleint, 1493 DR
Summer is coming to an end, and autumn will soon be upon us.

It has been some time since I last wrote here—about six months, I believe. Nearly as long as it has been since Mr. [[Tok Tok]] found me and nursed me back to health.

After the destruction of the Monastery of the Platinum Dawn, I was devastated and had nowhere left to go. That was when he offered me work at his tavern. I have lived and worked here ever since.

Life at [[The Wandering Tit]] was good.

My days were filled with enough tasks to keep my mind occupied. During the day, we cleaned the tavern and prepared the food and drink that would be served that evening. At night, I worked among the tables, bringing the customers their meals and ale.

The work has given me a new and considerably deeper appreciation for everything [[Harka]] once did for me.

During these months, I have also been blessed with new friends besides Mr. Tok Tok.

The first is [[Trixie]], a [[Half-Tabaxi]] who, I am told, has worked at the tavern since it opened. She performs for the crowd at night. I must admit that I have grown to appreciate the ability of mortals to weave stories into song.

Few among my kind would even bother to attempt such a thing. I can already hear [[Ashra]] declaring it beneath her, while [[Skaerth|Father]] would demand to know its practical value.

But I digress.

More recently, an insectoid woman named [[Mantis]] joined our little group. She belongs to a people I had never encountered before. I do not know the details of how she came here, and I have little desire to pry into either my companions’ pasts or Mr. Tok Tok’s decisions.

Regardless, she is a fine fighter.

I have sparred with her several times and remain surprised by how sturdy her body is, considering how thin her limbs appear. Granted, this judgment is based upon my current form. I cannot call it a new body anymore, but perhaps I would have held a different opinion before the curse.

In any case, I do not wish to spend too much ink recounting the past six months. Otherwise, I would need several additional entries, and given our present circumstances, that may prove difficult.

As for the events of today, everything began normally.

We followed our usual routine and shared a round of ale shortly before opening the tavern to the public. Until that moment, I had believed Mantis incapable of speech. We were therefore all surprised when we suddenly heard her speak... or, more precisely, when she communicated directly into our minds.

I had never experienced telepathy before. It was deeply strange.

Should I ever have another chance to speak with my big brother, I will have to compare my experience with what he encountered among the gith. I have heard that they possess psychic talents of their own.

Once we recovered from the surprise of discovering Mantis’s ability, we hurried to finish preparing the tavern. The evening proceeded normally for some time, aside from the usual collection of rude patrons.

One of them pulled my tail.

I have endured harassment before, but this was the first time one of those damned commoners dared lay his hands upon me in such a manner. I nearly roasted him alive. Had Mr. Tok Tok not intervened, I very well might have.

Even remembering it makes my blood boil.

For a mortal commoner to dare—

*Several lines of angry scribbling and deep scratches cut across the page.*

Enough.

I must focus before I lose the thread of the day’s events.

The evening continued according to its usual rhythm, and even Mr. Tok Tok performed for our patrons. His performances are quite different from Trixie’s. They combine music with physical acting, but rather than telling a story in the same manner as one of her songs, his movements seem intended to convey emotion.

This particular performance concerned a golem that wished to become a mortal of flesh and blood. It endured a number of strange adventures, including being swallowed by a whale, of all things.

When Mr. Tok Tok finished, it was Trixie’s turn.

She began as she normally does, but then her voice changed.

Her movements changed as well. She moved as though something else had taken hold of her body. The song she sang gave me goosebumps.

It still does.

I questioned her afterward, but she had no true memory of what had happened.

No sooner had the song ended than fire erupted around the tavern walls.

Blood-red fire.

My clan’s signature.

Two figures entered through the flames, both wearing cloaks of the same crimson hue, embroidered with golden thread. They appeared human, or at least like one of the other two-legged mortal peoples. There were no kobolds with them.

At first, I feared that Ashra or [[Infernia]] had found me—or worse, that Father himself had come.

The intruders gave me no useful answers. They knew who I was, but refused to reveal who had sent them. Whether they intended to capture me or kill me, I could not tell.

There were only two of them, and neither was accompanied by kobolds. That makes me suspect they were operatives stationed somewhere nearby rather than a force sent directly from the [[Sword Mountains]].

But that is only a suspicion.

They may have simply happened upon me. They may have been watching the tavern for days.

I do not know.

*Several tear stains spread across the page, thinning and smearing the ink.*

Once again, I have brought fire, death, and destruction upon those who offered me kindness.

Will my family ever allow me to live?

Or am I condemned to remain alone, lest I bring ruin upon everyone I dare to care for?

*The page has been crumpled, as though Azhura considered throwing it away before smoothing it out again.*

We fought the intruders.

One was killed. We dragged the other through a portal hidden in Mr. Tok Tok’s room. He told us that it led to a safe room.

When we emerged on the other side, we found ourselves in complete darkness. I truly miss being able to see in the dark.

Eventually, we managed to produce enough light to examine our surroundings. There was a door leading out of the room, and it stood partially open. This appeared to cause Mr. Tok Tok a great deal of concern.

We left the room to investigate.

I am still not certain how it happened, but Mr. Tok Tok lost the key to the portal. Then something appeared. I first thought it was a rodent, but that was not quite right.

It was something trying very hard to pretend that it was a rodent and failing miserably.

It had far too many legs.

The creature seized the key and began running across what I can only describe as cratered temple grounds. Mantis and Trixie attempted to catch it, but it slipped through their grasp.

The creature reached the far end of the grounds and ran toward a humanoid figure.

Then the space above us began to open.

I can describe it only as a vast maw—larger than the mouth of any living creature I have ever seen. I do not believe even Father’s jaws could compare.

As the maw opened, debris began falling from above and crashing into the temple grounds.

I suppose that explains the craters.

*The remaining lines dissolve into hurried, increasingly illegible scribbles.*

Today has been... hectic.

No. That is far too small a word.

Before I attempt a proper account, I should record our present condition. We are safe—mostly.

Grandma and Renn are missing.

None of us saw them disappear. None of us realized they were gone until far too late. I think my companions are carrying the same guilt, though none of us has had the strength to speak of it at length.

The rest of us have found a room that appears normal. At least, normal by the standards of this damned place. Its walls do not breathe. It has no tentacles crawling across the stone. No one has vanished while standing inside it.

For now, that is enough to make it our refuge.

I should begin from where yesterday’s account ended.

As we pursued the creature that stole Mr. Tok Tok’s portal key, we encountered two strangers.

One was a sailor and, judging by our brief conversation, apparently a captain. The other was a woman named Quinsley.

She was—and remains—something of a mystery.

I initially assumed they were enemies, perhaps allies of the creature carrying away our only reliable means of escape. In truth, they appeared to be trapped here just as we were. How they arrived remains unknown, but this hardly seemed the time to interrogate them about their pasts.

Quinsley appeared celestial, or at least touched by something celestial. I was openly hostile toward her at first. She responded by showing me an amulet bearing the symbol of Helm.

I could feel divine power within it.

My own pendant answered. The sensation was subtle, but unmistakable, as though the two objects recognized that they served powers standing upon the same side of some distant boundary.

That did not prove Quinsley harmless, but it made attacking her seem considerably less reasonable.

My companions had not shared my suspicion. Given the circumstances, perhaps that says more about me than it does about them.

We soon realized that the captain was ill. A recently bandaged wound covered one of his shoulders, and something appeared to be spreading outward from it. Even Quinsley’s healing magic could not stop it.

I believed it was poison.

I channeled the Platinum Dragon’s power into the wound. The captain seemed to improve, at least briefly, so we moved him back to the chamber where the surviving patrons and our captive were waiting.

We had barely resumed our search for the stolen key when the patrons called us back.

The corruption had returned with greater violence. It spread faster than before. I tried to cleanse it again, but the captain began convulsing beneath my hands.

I do not know whether my attempt provoked whatever was inside him or whether it had simply reached the next stage of its growth.

Either way, I could not save him.

The captain died.

Then the wound began to pulse.

Not twitch. Not spasm.

Pulse.

Something was moving beneath his flesh.

Even now, writing this, I can feel my hands beginning to shake.

I reopened the wound, first with my sword and then with a shorter blade that gave me better control. I tried to reach whatever had burrowed into him. Each time I came close, it forced itself deeper into the dead man’s body.

Then something emerged.

A worm, perhaps. Or a tentacle. It resembled the smaller growths infesting this place, though I cannot say whether they were truly related.

A second tendril tore its way out near the captain’s neck.

His head began pulling away from his shoulders.

The skin stretched first. Then the muscle. Then the spine began to separate, each vertebra cracking and drawing apart with a sound I would gladly carve from my memory.

At last, the head tore itself free.

And began to fly.

That was when the true horror began.

The creature screamed.

One moment I stood beside my companions. The next, they were gone.

I was surrounded by Father’s soldiers.

Kobolds pressed in from every side, armed and armored, ready to drag me back in chains. I could feel the mountain closing around me. I could already imagine the brand burning across my back and hear Father’s voice telling me that escape had only made the punishment worse.

I thought I had learned to recognize fear before it could master me.

I was wrong.

I struck the nearest kobold.

My blade sank into flesh.

The flying head passed beyond me, and the vision vanished with it.

There had been no kobold.

Mr. Tok Tok stood where the soldier had been, bright blood pouring across his chest.

My legs nearly failed beneath me.

The people hunting me had already taken his home and livelihood from him. Now my own hand had opened his flesh.

*Several lines have been violently scratched out. A few words remain visible: “saved me,” “my fault,” “forgive,” and “cannot lose another.”*

I had to stop writing.

I have calmed myself enough to continue.

Mr. Tok Tok and Miss Trixie eventually destroyed the head—or the thing controlling it. Looking back, I suspect the false rodent that stole the portal key may have been another creature of the same kind.

That is only a theory.

Once the immediate danger had passed, we treated the wounded and continued searching the surrounding wreckage. I found a strange candle whose flame burned blue.

We also discovered an iron key among the dead captain’s possessions. It opened the door through which the false rodent had escaped.

Quinsley strongly opposed continuing farther into the complex. I understood her objections. We had already lost one person, injured several others, and still knew almost nothing about where we were.

But our portal key—and perhaps our only way home—remained somewhere ahead.

After considerable argument, we decided to continue. We left the patrons and the bound cultist in the room where we had first arrived, believing it to be the safest place available.

Beyond the locked door, we entered what appeared to be the principal temple or sanctum of this place.

Trixie summoned a pseudodragon familiar to scout ahead.

The sight of the little creature stirred something unexpectedly painful in me. Familiarity, perhaps. Nostalgia. A longing for something I could not afford to consider while standing in that place.

I forced the feeling aside.

The pseudodragon explored effectively until it suddenly disappeared.

Before vanishing, it discovered a storage chamber containing a modest hoard. Among the stored goods stood a large crate filled with gold coins.

I walked toward it without consciously deciding to move.

The gold caught the light. For several moments, everything else in the room seemed distant and unimportant. I had nearly reached into the crate when a pounding sound came from the door behind us.

Then came the screaming.

The patrons we had left in the supposed safe room were gathered outside the door, terrified. They begged us to let them remain with us, claiming that something on the other side had been trying to reach them.

I directed them toward the storage chamber and hurried back for the captive cultist.

He was still unconscious. I carried him into the temple without difficulty, though I saw no sign of the creature the patrons claimed had attacked them.

By the time I returned, Grandma and Renn had disappeared.

Renn was the patron who had pulled my tail. I had little affection for him, and I doubt I ever would have developed any.

That does not make his disappearance acceptable.

What frightened me most was that no one could explain what had happened. The patrons had remained together. No one saw Grandma or Renn leave. No one heard them cry out.

They were simply gone.

Mantis helped me secure the prisoner to one of the storage shelves. I then joined the others in searching for any trace of the missing patrons.

That was when the gold caught my attention again.

I am ashamed to admit that this time I did not resist.

My hand was already reaching into the crate before I fully understood what I was doing. The familiar pull of it—the weight, the shine, the simple certainty of possession—drowned out nearly everything else.

Naturally, that was the moment the prisoner awoke.

He saw me.

Worse, he understood enough to mock me for it.

There are few humiliations more complete than being lectured on one’s weaknesses by a cultist while one’s hand is halfway into a crate of coins.

I could feel the heat rising into my face. I withdrew my hand and attempted to cover the embarrassment with anger.

The anger, at least, was genuine.

Before he could continue mocking me, I demanded to know who had sent him.

I intended to shout.

What emerged was something deeper, harsher, and considerably less human than I had intended—a single roar carrying every measure of anger, humiliation, and fear I had been restraining since the tavern burned.

I did not look at my companions afterward.

I am uncertain what they now suspect.

The prisoner named Halvern.

He may have been lying.

I do not think he was.

If Halvern sent only two agents, he may not have been certain that I was truly at the Wandering Tit. Perhaps he intended to confirm my presence before informing the clan. Perhaps he believed two servants would be enough.

Or perhaps the years he has spent tormenting me have taught him nothing except contempt.

Whatever his reasoning, he will use the destruction of the tavern to his advantage. If we escape this place, we must move quickly.

Otherwise—

*A small puncture and several creases mark the page, as though a claw pressed into the parchment.*

Infernia may already be on her way.

Or Ashra.

I do not know which thought is worse.

*A small greasy stain crosses the next few lines, apparently left while the writer was eating.*

We are now gathered inside a chamber that appears to serve as both a studio and a bedroom. It is modest, and we fit inside only barely, but it remains the safest room we have encountered.

At least its walls do not appear to be alive.

We found several loose pages from another journal. They belonged to someone named Ashyra Veil. We believe that a portrait formerly displayed outside this room depicted her.

She possessed a strange beauty. There was something unsettling about the image, though I cannot identify what disturbed me.

According to the surviving pages, Lady Veil sought apotheosis.

Mortals are remarkably eager to seize powers whose price they cannot comprehend. Halvern suffers from the same blindness. He torments me in pursuit of whatever reward Father has promised him, apparently never considering what such a reward would cost.

Lady Veil may have intended to become a deity herself. Alternatively, she may have tried to awaken something and steal its power.

Either possibility was foolish.

The journal also suggests that this place is somehow connected to an astral dreadnought.

Fascinating creatures.

Mother once told me what little she knew about them after reading letters Val sent during his service among the gith. I remember her trying to explain how something could swim through the Astral Sea while consuming nearly anything unfortunate enough to cross its path.

I had hoped never to verify those stories personally.

If we have interpreted the journal correctly, the dreadnought has been feeding for gods know how long. The debris scattered across the cratered grounds may be the remains of things it swallowed.

Lady Veil appears to have collected material from its victims and fed it to another creature—a godling, perhaps.

The name written in the journal was Moander.

I do not recognize it. I can only pray that Bahamut grants me some understanding before ignorance becomes fatal.

Whatever Lady Veil brought into this place eventually consumed her, just as it consumed the sacrifices she offered.

One passage appears to contain her final warning:

The moon is haunted.

I do not know what that means.

I am increasingly afraid that we will learn.

The day’s events have exhausted everyone. We agreed to rest here before continuing.

I brought the crate of gold into the room and arranged it as a bed.

It is not a particularly impressive amount. The crate looked far more promising before I examined its contents properly.

Still, I had forgotten how comforting that weight could feel beneath me.

I do not intend to examine that thought tonight.

## 13 Eleint, 1493 DR
*The handwriting below is hurried and uneven. Several letters trail into trembling lines.*

Something was inside this room.

Something nearly killed me.

It appeared as darkness deeper than the Abyss, a shape that seemed to consume what little light surrounded it.

It had eyes.

At least, I think they were eyes.

By the Platinum Flame, they looked through me as though my body were nothing more than smoke.

It lifted me by the neck.

No one else appeared able to see it. I do not know whether it revealed itself only to me or whether it was never truly visible at all. I could feel its fingers tightening. I can still feel the tender marks they left beneath my jaw.

I do not know how I escaped.

When I could move again, I reached for the blue candle, hoping its flame might expose or drive away the darkness.

The candle was not where I expected it to be.

Instead, I found a rusted key inside my own backpack.

I believe it may open the door leading into the main chamber.

I did not place it there.

I do not think I will sleep again tonight.

May the Platinum Flame guide us.

May it protect those I have failed to protect.

And may it reveal whatever is watching us before it decides to strike again.

I had hoped that my next entry would be written on the morning of the 14th. Alas, far too much has happened in far too little time, and I would be remiss if I allowed exhaustion to steal the details from my memory.

To resume where I left off: After the shadow attacked me, I discovered the rusted key inside my pack. We believed it might open the way into the next chamber.

We attempted to wake Trixie. She would not wake.

She breathed. Her heart still beat. But no amount of shaking or calling her name brought any response. I hesitate to call it a coma because I have no idea what has actually happened to her, but I can think of no better word.

Perhaps something in this place reached her as that shadow reached me. Perhaps it is connected to her song. Perhaps neither is true. Regardless, mortal bodies are terribly fragile things.

We argued over what to do next. Considering what had attacked me and Trixie's condition, pushing farther into this place seemed increasingly foolish.

Mantis apparently disagreed.

Without warning, she took the rusted key from my hand and rushed toward the closed door.

"Mantis!"

She opened it and went through.

Mr. Tok Tok, Quinsley, and I followed immediately, leaving the patrons behind with instructions to remain where they were and watch over Trixie.

We caught Mantis quickly enough. Unfortunately, by then we were already inside.

The chamber beyond was dark, wet, and warm. There were noises around us: soft movements, distant contractions, the occasional sound of something dragging itself across something else.

For several moments, I had the terrible impression that we had walked into the body of some enormous creature. Then I cast light upon my sword.

I almost wished I had not.

The architecture was twisted into shapes that seemed less built than grown. The walls and floor shifted subtly beneath layers of tendrils and pulsing tissue. Tentacles disappeared into gaps between structures that looked almost like ribs.

It reminded me faintly of illustrations of illithid architecture in a book Val once brought back from a gith library. Faintly.

Those structures had been alien.

This place was diseased.

A sound above the doorway made us turn. The false rat was hanging over the entrance, but we were already too far from the door when it dropped to the floor... and grew.

Its little body twisted outward, splitting and expanding until there were two tentacled monstrosities where the pathetic imitation of a rodent had been.

The door slammed shut behind them and then they screamed, the sound reminded me of claws dragged slowly across slate, only loud enough that I felt it inside my teeth. We barely had time to recover before one of the creatures spat a thick, viscous fluid directly onto Mr. Tok Tok.

I caught only the edge of the smell from where I stood, and my eyes immediately began watering. I swear upon Bahamut that the wyvern waste pits back in my old march smelled like roses compared with that filth.

The second creature struck at me with a tentacle. I managed to evade the main blow and knock the limb aside with my tail. Even that brief contact nearly threw me off balance.

Whatever these things were, they were strong. Once the surprise passed, however, we answered.

Mantis attacked first.

I have sparred with her. I already knew she was capable. Apparently I had underestimated her. All four of her arms began moving at once. She hacked, tore, struck, and cut through the creature with such efficiency that for several moments the best description I can offer is that she became a living meat grinder.

Quinsley called upon Helm but something went wrong. I cannot say whether Helm could not hear her, whether the surrounding corruption interfered with the prayer, or whether some other force resisted her. I only know that the divine response was not what she expected.

I could feel something wrong with the air around us, something twisted where divine power should have flowed cleanly. That realization did little for my confidence.

Still, if Mantis and Quinsley were willing to fight, I could hardly stand behind them.

I focused upon the creature before me and called upon the Platinum Flame. A sigil burned into existence upon the abomination. It would not escape me, not this time. 

You are mine now, I thought with a grin on my face.

I drew a deep breath. Flame gathered in my chest. For a heartbeat, I felt the familiar pressure building, then the brand across my back burned. It felt as though someone had pressed heated iron directly into the old scar. My breath caught, and the flames escaped in a ragged burst long before I intended to release them.

It was pathetic compared with what I wanted but it was enough. The creature burned and it screamed again... louder.

I managed to remain standing. Whether through Bahamut's grace, stubbornness, or simple terror, I cannot say. My ears rang so badly that I had to clench my jaw to keep myself focused. Another scream like that might have broken what little concentration I had left.

Then both creatures spat.

This time the filth struck me directly across the face. There are experiences I intend to erase from memory through repeated bathing. This is one of them.

I gagged so hard I could barely breathe. My eyes poured tears. Mr. Tok Tok had been struck again as well, though Mantis and Quinsley remained clean enough to keep fighting.

While I struggled to see, the rear wall began to move. At first I thought another chamber was opening, then Grandma emerged, followed by Renn. 

They were both... smiling. But, but their smile was wrong: Too wide, too still! They looked like puppets being reminded how a face was supposed to work.

I barely had time to call out before their necks opened and let out the same terrible scream sound as the other creatures, then then their bodies began to collapse.

Skin sagged. Muscle separated.

Their forms rotted apart before our eyes until neither of them resembled anything that had once sat inside the Wandering Tit. The remains flowed together.

I do not know whether those truly were Grandma and Renn, whether something had hollowed them out and worn their bodies, or whether the place had simply learned their shapes.

Whatever they were, they became one mass of flesh that pressed itself into the rear wall and opened into an enormous glowing eye.

We should probably have paid more attention to the eye, in retrospect, that seems obvious. However, at the time, the two screaming horrors directly in front of us felt somewhat more urgent.

Mantis continued carving through the creature on the right. Quinsley supported both of us as best she could, alternating between attacks and keeping us on our feet. I focused everything I could still call from Bahamut into my strikes. My blade burned with radiant fire as it tore into the creature before me.

The fight became a blur of tentacles, steel, prayer, shrieking, and that gods-damned smell.

Then an dark, emerald beam crossed the chamber, striking Mr. Tok Tok directly in the chest. I expected blood; instead, the impact exploded into splinters... wooden splinters and sap.

For several moments, I simply stared.

Mr. Tok Tok's performance from the tavern returned to me—the golem who wished to become flesh and blood.

I had thought it was a story.

I am beginning to suspect that some of Mr. Tok Tok's stories are not stories at all. Regardless this was not the time to ask.

We were barely holding the creatures in front of us, something behind us had just fired a beam capable of tearing through Mr. Tok Tok's chest, and the giant eye apparently had opinions about our continued existence.

Then the eye disappeared back into the wall, I allowed myself exactly one breath of relief... that is until the ground split open and a face crawled out... no... two faces sharing one horrible, gaping mouth! The two-faced abomination pulled itself free of the floor, twisted and malformed as though someone had attempted to smash to heads together into one. 

It's horrific maw yawned open, and it threw up... no, *birthed* two twisted, sinewy, bloody humanoid creatures, like animated meat puppets crafted of entrails.

*A short phrase is written beside: Skarrenda i infernalis.*

One of the face's eyes began glowing with the same dark emerald light as the larger eye before.

Mantis moved before the rest of us, she seized Mr. Tok Tok with two of her arms, and continued fighting with the others. As I mentioned before, I was quite impressed with her performance.

Between her attacks, Quinsley's support, and what strength I had left, we finally brought down the two larger tentacled creatures blocking the door.

We had almost created enough room to retreat when the humanoid things lashed out. Tentacles wrapped around Mantis and Quinsley, and for one terrible moment, I thought both of them were about to be dragged away. However, they fought back and freed themselves. 

Somewhere in the struggle, Quinsley found Mr. Tok Tok's missing portal key the mess left behind by the tentacled creatures. The actual key. The one that damned false rat had stolen. We looked at each other for a split second, and that was enough to make a decision without speaking out loud. 

We ran.

Mantis went first, carrying Mr. Tok Tok as she raced toward the courtyard through the hoard chamber. Quinsley took the other route through the studio, shouting for the patrons to follow.

Initially, Quinsley tried to lift Trixie to carry her to safety. However, the half-orc among the patrons lifted the still-unconscious Trixie and carried her himself. However, the halfling did not move, he simply stood there, frozen.

I passed him at a run.

Then stopped.

Damn it.

I doubled back just enough to hook my tail around him and pulled him bodily along behind me. I had never realized my tail could manage something that precise. Despite everything happening around us, I actually smiled... Only for a moment.

Then the corridor turned green, another emerald flash filled everything ahead of me. A heartbeat later came the sound of thunder and destruction.

Stone shattered around the exit as the beam punched through the closed door and the surrounding walls, striking directly where everyone had been gathering.

After what I had seen it do to Mr. Tok Tok, I expected to find bodies, I feared the worst.

I ran toward them. But before I arrived the humanoid creatures came crawling after us, blocking me from reaching my companions in the courtyard.

I prayed. Not for victory. Not for vengeance.

Just once, I begged Bahamut to let me overcome the curse. Just once. Long enough to reach them. Long enough to get everyone out. I turned the corner and... 

Most of them were alive. I cannot properly describe the relief of seeing my companions moving toward the obelisk.

The half-orc who had carried Trixie was not among the fortunate. I hope he found his way safely to the Fugue Plane. I barely knew him but he still deserved better than this.

That said, the others were running... and then a second realization reached me.

They had left me behind... THEY HAD LEFT ME BEHIND!

I admit that perhaps this was not the most rational interpretation of events. At the time, however, I was standing alone in a corridor full of monsters while everyone I knew was rapidly becoming smaller in the distance.

Rationality was not my strongest quality; I breathed fire again, be it because of my rage or earlier prayers, the flames answered properly this time and both creatures burned.

I tried to force my way through them while shouting several things at my companions which, upon reflection, were probably not suitable language for a servant of Bahamut.

Perhaps they heard me, perhaps they simply noticed I was missing; whatever the case, Mantis turned around first, then Mr. Tok Tok and finally Quinsley.

All three came back together, and we finished the creatures.

Then we ran. Gods, we ran. I do not believe I have run like that since the fall of the Monastery of the Platinum Dawn.

By the time we reached the obelisk, the portal in the ground was already open, so we threw ourselves through it. The last of us had barely crossed when the far side of the obelisk disappeared beneath another burst of emerald light.

Stone exploded, thunder followed. The beam passed across the space where the portal had been. Finally, the gateway closed and everything became still.

We were back, but the world outside had changed considerably in two days. 

The Wandering Tit was gone. Where there had once been warm light, crowded tables, spilled ale, Trixie's music, and Mr. Tok Tok's strange performances, there was now only blackened timber and ash.

That's when we realized that there was smoke rose farther away toward the settlement, and there were figures moving in the distance.

I cannot tell who they are from here.

I am afraid to guess.

## 14 Eleint, 1493 DR

I'm writing from the magic shop in the early morning of the 14th of Eleint. We are safe, at least for now... at least until light comes and day breaks, but for the moment, the shadows are our refuge. Now, let me narrate the events of this past day, for they have been weighing heavily on my mind and conscience.

Smoke. That was the first thing waiting for us after escaping that damned place.

Smoke, ash, and beneath it something faintly familiar that I wish I did not recognize so easily—the scent of burned flesh.

In the distance, three figures moved along the road, all of them wearing crimson-red cloaks: a color I was far too familiar with, the colors of my family.

After everything inside the dreadnought—or temple, or whatever it truly was—I had allowed myself to believe that returning here would at least mean returning to something familiar. Instead, we had apparently escaped one nightmare only to crawl directly into another.

We had not gone far before someone approached us. It took us a moment to recognize [[Iris Bellwether|Iris]], the town's goblin mail carrier. She had been at the inn a few times, but I had never had a good opportunity to speak with her directly. At that moment, however, she looked less like someone delivering letters and more like someone who had spent the last day desperately trying not to be noticed.

She apprised us of the situation, explaining how the Crimson Flame Clan had arrived almost from nowhere and how people had seen a dragon flying over the town.

Then she said something that made my heart sink.

A woman had arrived in town.

A dragon lady.

[[Ashra]].

I could feel my heart beating faster, and my breath became ragged and shallow. I could even feel my eyes opening wide as I tried to take everything in, desperately searching for her and for somewhere to hide.

I had to start telling myself to breathe and calm down, actively focusing on keeping control of myself before the fear had a chance to overtake me.

Breathe.

Think.

Do not panic.

Do not let her see you panic.

Unfortunately, I was not entirely successful in calming myself, but I was successful enough to follow Iris and the others into the town smithy... the smithy that had belonged to [[Renn Vask|Renn]].

I tried not to think too much about that. As annoying as he had been, I still wished I could have saved him.

Once we were inside, Iris explained what had happened while we were gone.

The cultists had seized the town council and were holding its members hostage. Their threat was simple: one member of the Circle would burn each day until the town surrendered the woman Ashra had come looking for... a dragonblood woman who lived here.

Me.

It was happening again. This was the [[Monastery of the Platinum Dawn]] all over again, except this time there were no other paladins or knights ready to put up a fight... only innocent civilians.

And it was again all my fault.

Iris kept explaining that we had until the end of tomorrow. If I had not been surrendered to Ashra by then, according to her threat, the entire town would burn to the ground.

I wish I could believe surrender would prevent that, but I know my sister better. The moment I surrender myself to her, she will still burn everything to the ground and take whatever treasures remain for her hoard. Once a dragon raid has begun, it is very difficult to stop it.

I would know. I participated in far too many of them over the years. I told the others as much... and I also finally told them exactly who Ashra is.

My eldest sister. An adult red dragon.

I had perhaps assumed more of this was obvious than it actually was. Iris certainly had not known; the poor girl went pale enough that for a moment I thought she might faint.

That said, my companions were not particularly surprised by what I told them. Some of them were even interested in fighting the cultists—and my sister—which would have been suicidal.

Fortunately, no one was foolish enough to actually attempt it. Thanks be to the [[Bahamut|Platinum Flame]] for that.

According to Iris, Ashra spends the day within the town but withdraws at night to a camp outside it. That gave us a window, allowing us to rest until the early evening, when apparently she had left. Then we began discussing what to do next.

My first thought was evacuation. Get everyone who could move into the [[Misty Forest]]. Scatter them before Ashra realizes what is happening. If necessary, I could remain behind and draw her attention while the others escaped.

I said as much, but Mr. [[Tok Tok]], [[Quinsley]], and Iris objected immediately.

Apparently, while Ashra and the cultists had been tightening their grip on the settlement, some of the locals had begun organizing resistance from the brewery. Iris offered to guide us through a back route that was not being watched.

Once we were certain Ashra had left town, we started toward the brewery.

That was when [[Mantis]] found the tracks.

Thri-kreen. Her people.

She became tense almost immediately and said something that concerned me considerably:

"They found me."

For a moment I thought she meant my family; given recent events, I believe I can be forgiven for assuming every ominous statement somehow concerns my family. For good or ill, however, that was not the case this time. Whatever was after Mantis belonged to her past. She did not explain further, and I did not press.

I have spent months appreciating that my companions do not demand answers from me before I am prepared to give them. It would be rather hypocritical to deny Mantis the same courtesy.

The tracks followed roughly along the route we needed to take, which presented us with an unpleasant choice: we could either abandon the hidden path and risk moving closer to Crimson Flame patrols, or continue and possibly meet another Thri-kreen whose intentions we were uncertain of.

We chose to continue down the secret path, and we did not have to wonder for long whether the stranger was friendly, as something struck Mantis hard enough to tear into her thorax.

A strange curved weapon came spinning from the trees and returned toward its wielder. Then another followed. And another.

The hunter remained hidden among the branches while focusing almost entirely upon Mantis. Whatever history existed between them, it did not appear affectionate.

The fight quickly became less a battle and more a pursuit through the forest.

Mantis charged after the hunter. Mr. Tok Tok followed with considerably more speed than I expected from him. [[Trixie]] hurled those strange crimson bolts of hers between the trees, while Quinsley attempted to slow the creature with divine magic.

And then there was Jimothy.

I have not yet written about him, mostly because I am still trying to decide what Jimothy actually is. All I can say with certainty is that Trixie summoned him, and that he resembles a small woodland creature which, under different circumstances, I would have assumed had wandered too close to one of the wyvern enclosures back in my march and was about to become lunch.

Instead, he threw himself directly at the hunter and began biting pieces out of him.

Repeatedly.

The durability of the Thri-kreen continues to surprise me. Mortals though they may be, they are remarkably difficult to kill.

I had sparred with Mantis enough to know that her people were sturdy, but until then I had not understood exactly how sturdy.

The hunter endured wounds that would have left most creatures bleeding helplessly into the leaves. Mantis cut through pieces of his carapace. Mr. Tok Tok managed to break through it with his strikes. Jimothy kept finding exposed places to sink his teeth. Still, the hunter kept moving.

He retreated deeper into the trees, using his weapons to deflect attacks and trying repeatedly to put distance between himself and us.

I marked him with Bahamut's sigil and joined the pursuit, throwing one of my javelins after him.

I missed. Badly.

I am beginning to think I need considerably more practice with the things. It was honestly rather pathetic that I could not land a single one, though fortunately no one else was standing where mine landed.

Our pursuit was not quiet, though. Branches broke. Weapons struck trees. Mantis moved through the undergrowth with all the subtlety one might expect from four-armed warriors trying to murder one another at speed.

Eventually, we heard movement nearby and saw far too late that a Crimson Flame patrol archer had come to investigate the noise.

Wonderful.

We had gone from attempting to secretly contact a resistance movement to fighting Mantis's mysterious pursuer while one of my family's soldiers wandered directly toward us.

I attempted to salvage the situation by invoking the force of my oath and commanding the archer to ignore what he had seen and leave us.

He resisted.

Today is truly not my day.

Quinsley responded more effectively by striking him with divine light, and from that point forward, everything became confused.

The hunter tried to escape, but we pursued him relentlessly. Mantis carved through his armor whenever she could reach him. Mr. Tok Tok kept pace somehow, moving through the forest faster than I had ever seen him move, while Trixie's magic continued eating away at the creature from a distance.

And Jimothy—

Gods preserve me.

Jimothy finally caught the hunter at a point where Mantis had already torn open the armor around his neck. The little beast buried his teeth into the exposed flesh, digging deep until the hunter stopped moving.

Whatever Mantis's people train themselves to endure, there are apparently limits after all.

With the hunter finally dead, the rest of us were free to turn our full attention toward the Crimson Flame archer.

What followed was considerably less elegant than I would prefer to record in detail. Quinsley burned him with divine magic while Trixie continued tearing into him with those crimson bolts of hers. Mantis, meanwhile—and only Bahamut knows how—arrived carrying one of the dead hunter's weapons and immediately joined the attack.

Mr. Tok Tok threw several projectiles at the man. The most spectacular of these was a cabbage. Yes. A cabbage.

He crossed an absurd distance through the trees, landed in a vegetable patch, grabbed a cabbage, and threw it directly at the cultist's head. And, astonishingly, it struck him squarely. Unfortunately, the cabbage had begun to spoil and therefore lacked the necessary structural integrity to be a particularly effective weapon. I do not know whether to be disappointed or impressed.

The archer fought desperately once he realized he was surrounded. He managed to wound Quinsley and even put an arrow into Jimothy, who immediately retreated behind my wings.

The little creature waited there until an opportunity presented itself, then launched himself from behind me and tore into the archer's throat.

That was the end of the fight: two enemies dead and our attempt at stealth considerably less successful than intended.

Fortunately, Ashra remained blissfully unaware—or at least I pray she remained unaware—that her little sister was crawling through the bushes murdering her patrols.

We dragged the archer's body into a nearby magic shop before anyone else could discover it. That was where we met [[Fen|Fen]], who immediately warned us about his doorknob. Apparently, it bites.

I am far too tired, and far too concerned about Ashra, to worry about such trifling matters at present.

Another Crimson Flame archer came around the corner before we had properly hidden the evidence, as there was still far too much blood outside. Fen saw the patrolman approaching and immediately claimed that the blood belonged to him.

And... somehow, this worked.

The archer looked at the old man, looked at the blood, muttered something about old fools, and left.

So, somehow, we are still alive.

The resistance is somewhere ahead of us. The Circle remains captive. Mantis's people, or at least one of them, have apparently found her. But perhaps more importantly...

Ashra is here.

And if she discovers I am here before we are ready—

*Several deep marks score the parchment before the sentence resumes.*

No. I will not give her that satisfaction.

Not again.

## 15 Eleint, 1493 DR

We spent the remainder of the night in [[Fen|Fen]]'s magic shop. After the battle with the thri-kreen and the archer, none of us were in any condition to continue through the town, especially with [[Ashra]] expected to return by morning.

We woke before dawn.

[[Trixie]] sent an imp to scout the area and learn more about the [[Crimson Flame]] forces occupying the town. While it searched, we prepared to reach the brewery and contact the resistance. If possible, we would help evacuate the townspeople into the forest before Ashra could carry out her threat to burn the town.

Fen offered us several enchanted items to use. I took the [[Boots of Elvenkind]], which helped make my footsteps quieter. I figured I could use the help with my stealth, since my armor and body are difficult not to notice.

We somehow avoided the patrols and made our way toward the brewery. [[Quinsley]] nearly drew the attention of one of the guards, but Trixie distracted him with her imp while I used my amulet to change my clothing into something better suited to the darkness.

After some effort, we finally reached the brewery and entered through a window at the back. [[Brynn Thatcher|Brynn]], its owner, found us there and led us into the basement.

I had high hopes that the people of the town—this resistance—would be of significant help in fighting off my former clan.

I was sorely disappointed. The resistance was smaller than I had imagined.

There was [[Oswin Marrow|Oswin]], the owner of the grocery store, and only one other person. Certainly not enough to fight the Crimson Flame, much less Ashra.

Oswin confronted us about [[Garrek]], his adopted son, who had died aboard the dreadnought while we survived. [[Tobin]] spoke about the people we had lost, and his words affected everyone in the room, especially Oswin. He left the basement, carrying his grief and anger with him.

I could barely contain my own guilt; their deaths were on me. I may not have caused every danger we faced, but I brought that danger to their door. I will not allow more people to die for my sake if I can prevent it.

I went after Oswin. The least I could do was take responsibility and buy the villagers some time to evacuate. However, Oswin came up with a different plan instead.

He explained that the town had once been occupied by the [[Verdant Claw]], an organization connected to the [[Cult of the Dragon]] and supported by a green dragon, Chuth. The resistance had survived that occupation by using tunnels beneath the town to move unseen and strike from unexpected places.

According to Fen, the Verdant Claw had been working with the Cult in an attempt to bring [[Tiamat]] out of [[Avernus]]. I found it fascinating that his account resembled the stories [[Skaerth|Father]] used to tell us, though Father naturally placed more emphasis on dragons and the failures of mortals.

In the end, we agreed to retrieve an arcane focus belonging to the town's former hero: a shield that had helped save the settlement from the green dragon. Afterward, we would attempt to rescue the last remaining kidnapped member of the Circle, a  [[Vantha Emberlight|dragonborn woman ]] who had served as one of the town's leaders.

The tunnels led us to a chamber filled with old beds and a statue of an angelic warrior. The statue carried the shield, but it also wore a helmet and pauldron that seemed to possess a divine connection to [[Helm]].

Quinsley put on the helmet.

She was immediately overcome by a vision. Darkness gathered around her, and she cried out the name [[Neronvain]]. A shadowy figure appeared, wearing a distorted, draconic helmet.

I remember little of the battle beyond the final strike. I lunged from one of the beds, nearly lost my balance, and recovered with the help of my wings and tail. I brought my sword around in a backswing and let the power of the [[Bahamut|Platinum Flame]] burn through the blade.

The apparition dissolved into dark smoke.

Afterward, we searched the room, where I found a small wooden bird.

*Some fast, passing scribbles, clearly Azhura's writing but at the same time distinct from her normal calligraphy and style: **Gold! There was gold, my hoard, I needed it, it... it calls to me!***

I lost control.

Quinsley found a pouch of gold, and the sight of the coins awakened something in me, something old and instinctive. I tried to take them from her. When she said she was not trying to hoard them, memories of my own hoard came rushing back: my lair, my servants, my territory, and everything that had been taken from me!

Thanks be to [[Tok Tok|Mr. Tok Tok]], who used some kind of spell to bring me back to myself. Otherwise, I do not want to imagine what could have happened.

I am so ashamed. I do not know how else to express the disappointment I feel toward myself. I thought the curse had taken nearly everything from me that made me a dragon, but apparently, it left more behind than I realized.

Quinsley had done nothing wrong, and yet I had frightened her over a handful of coins. We reconciled, but I cannot pretend the incident did not trouble me.

We continued through the tunnels, and took a beat to surface near the burned remains of the Wandering Tits. There, we noticed a faint, sweet smell drifting from the direction of the cemetery. We left it unexplored for the moment, returning to the tunnels and leaving the shield to the mage before continuing toward the captive member of the Circle.

Not long afterward, we heard fighting.

In a large chamber where several corridors met, we found [[Iris]], Tobin, and Brynn fighting six creatures that looked like oversized mixtures of bats and mosquitoes. We joined the battle and managed to defeat them before anyone was killed.

While searching the remains, Trixie found another pouch of gold, and the same instinct rose in me again. However, I was somehow able to recognize it before I lost control.

I dug my claws into myself until the pain cleared my thoughts. It worked. I did not attack her. It is a small victory, but I will take it.

Ashra may return at any moment. The town remains occupied, the last member of the Circle is still captive, and the resistance is preparing to move against forces far greater than itself.
